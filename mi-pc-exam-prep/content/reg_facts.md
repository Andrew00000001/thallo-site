# reg: Insurance Regulation

Statute text: Michigan Compiled Laws via legislature.mi.gov (complete through PA 103 of 2026), read October 2026. "Director" and "commissioner" both mean the DIFS Director.

## Company Regulation: Producer Appointment

- A producer may not act as an insurer's agent unless the insurer has appointed the producer. A producer who is not acting as the insurer's agent does not need an appointment. [MCL 500.1208a(1)]
- A producer may not **bind coverage** for an insurer unless that insurer has appointed the producer. [MCL 500.1208a(2)]
- The insurer files a notice of appointment within **15 days** of whichever comes first: the date the agency contract is executed, or the date the first insurance application is submitted. One filing can appoint the producer to all or some insurers in the insurer's holding-company group. [MCL 500.1208a(3)]
- The director verifies eligibility within a reasonable time, **not more than 30 days**. If the producer is ineligible, the director notifies the insurer within **5 days** of that determination. [MCL 500.1208a(4)]
- The insurer pays the appointment fee and renewal appointment fee: **$5 per producer per year**, resident or nonresident. [MCL 500.1208a(5); MCL 500.240(1)(c)]
- Managing general agents (MGAs): the insurer must give the commissioner written notice within **30 days** of entering into or terminating an MGA contract. The insurer must also review the MGA's operations on site at least **semiannually** and check its books **quarterly** to see whether any agent has become an MGA. [MCL 500.1411(c),(e),(f)]
- An agent may not, during any 12-month period, place insurance on his or her own risks, or the risks of employees, employer or business associates, totaling more than **15%** of the total premium the agent placed in that period. This is the controlled-business limit. [MCL 500.1208]

## Company Regulation: Termination of Appointment

- An insurer that terminates a producer **for a 1239 cause** must notify the commissioner within **30 days after the termination's effective date**. The same applies when the insurer knows a court or regulator found that the producer engaged in 1239 conduct. On written request, the insurer must supply more information. [MCL 500.1208b(1)]
- A termination for **any other reason** also requires notice to the commissioner within **30 days after the effective date**. [MCL 500.1208b(2)]
- If the insurer later learns of information that would have been reportable, it must notify the commissioner promptly. [MCL 500.1208b(3)]
- Within **15 days** after notifying the commissioner, the insurer mails a copy of the notice to the producer's last known address. For a for-cause termination, the copy goes by **certified mail, return receipt requested**, or by nationally recognized **overnight delivery**. [MCL 500.1208b(4)]
- The producer may file written comments with the commissioner within **30 days** after receiving the notice. The producer must send a copy to the insurer at the same time. [MCL 500.1208b(4)]
- Anyone who reports a termination is immune from civil liability **unless they acted with actual malice**. [MCL 500.1208b(5)]
- An insurer or producer that fails to report as required may have its license or certificate of authority suspended or revoked and may be fined under 1244. [MCL 500.1246(6)]
- After a termination, a producer who has property rights in the renewals stays responsible until the existing policies are canceled, replaced or expire. If the written agreement does not address this period, the producer may **service existing policies**. The producer may **not bind new risks, renew policies, or increase the insurer's obligation** without the insurer's approval. [MCL 500.1209(1)]
- An insurer may not cancel or nonrenew an insured's policy **because the producer's contract was terminated**. [MCL 500.1209(1)]
- **Auto and home insurers** may terminate a producer's authority for auto or home insurance only for:
  - malfeasance
  - breach of fiduciary duty or trust
  - violation of the Insurance Code
  - failure to perform under the contract
  - submitting **fewer than 25 applications** for home and auto insurance in the preceding 12 months

  [MCL 500.1209(2)]
- An auto or home termination may **not** be based primarily on the geographic location of the producer's business, its loss experience tied to that location, or the producer's performance of chapter 21 (Essential Insurance Act) obligations. [MCL 500.1209(3)]
- These protections do not apply to employee producers or captive producers (those representing only 1 insurer or affiliated group) when the insurer owns the renewal rights **and** the termination does not cause any home or auto policy to be canceled or nonrenewed. [MCL 500.1209(4)]

## Producer Regulation: Duties and Who Must Be Licensed

- A person may not sell, solicit or negotiate insurance in Michigan for any line without a license for that line. [MCL 500.1201a(1)]
- Key definitions [MCL 500.1201]:
  - **Insurance producer**: a person required to be licensed to sell, solicit or negotiate insurance. "Agent" means insurance producer.
  - **Sell**: to exchange a contract of insurance for money or its equivalent on behalf of an insurer.
  - **Solicit**: to attempt to sell, or to ask or urge a person to apply for a particular kind of insurance from a particular company.
  - **Negotiate**: to confer directly with, or advise directly, a purchaser or prospective purchaser about a particular contract's substantive benefits, terms or conditions.
- A license does **not** by itself give the holder actual, apparent or inherent authority to represent or commit an insurer. [MCL 500.1201(h)]
- **Agent of the insurer**: represents the insurer, not the insured. **Agent of the insured**: a producer **not appointed** by the insurer on the placed policy; treated as representing the insured. [MCL 500.1201(b),(c)]
- No producer license is needed for:
  - insurer officers, directors or employees who receive **no commission** and do executive, administrative, managerial or clerical work; underwriting, loss control or claims work; or special-agent technical assistance to producers
  - persons whose only insurance sales are rental-car insurance incidental to a rental of **90 days or less**, or portable electronics, travel, or self-storage property insurance (stored property valued up to **$10,000**)
  - salaried full-time employees advising their own employer without commission

  [MCL 500.1202(2)]
- The insurer itself does not need a producer license. [MCL 500.1202(1)]

## Producer Regulation: Commissions

- An insurer or producer may not pay a commission, service fee or other valuable consideration to a person who must be licensed but is not. [MCL 500.1240(1)]
- An unlicensed person who must be licensed may not accept such payment. [MCL 500.1240(2)]
- **Renewal and deferred commissions** may be paid if the person was licensed **at the time of the sale**. [MCL 500.1240(3)]
- Commissions may be paid or assigned to an **insurance agency**, or to persons who do not sell, solicit or negotiate, unless the payment would be an illegal rebate under 2024. [MCL 500.1240(4)]
- DIFS allows an unlicensed person to be paid a referral fee for leads only if the fee is not contingent on the referral applying for or buying a policy. An unlicensed person may also receive an override if he or she does not sell, solicit or negotiate insurance. [MCL 500.1240(4); DIFS Compensation & Rebating FAQ: Referral Fees, Override Commissions]
- An agent may not reward or remunerate any person for procuring or inducing business, furnishing leads or prospects, or otherwise acting as an agent. Exceptions: licensed solicitors and agent-to-agent contracts under 1211 and 1212, and placing refused coverage through another licensed agent under 1207(12). A solicitor likewise may not reward or remunerate anyone for procuring business, furnishing leads or prospects, or acting as an agent or solicitor. DIFS still allows a producer to pay an unlicensed person a referral fee for leads if the fee is not contingent on the referral applying for or buying a policy. [MCL 500.1207(11); MCL 500.1216(3); DIFS Compensation & Rebating FAQ: Referral Fees]
- Medicare-eligible disability policies: the first-year commission may not exceed the commission paid in each of the next **2 renewal years**. [MCL 500.2011(1)]

## Types of Licensees: Producer (Lines of Authority)

Individual producer lines [MCL 500.1206(1)]:

- **Life**: life insurance, including endowment and annuity benefits. May include accidental death or dismemberment and disability income.
- **Accident and health or sickness**: sickness, bodily injury or accidental death. May include disability income.
- **Property**: direct or consequential loss of or damage to property of every kind.
- **Casualty**: legal liability, including for death, injury, disability, or damage to real or personal property.
- **Variable life and variable annuity products**.
- **Personal lines**: property and casualty coverage sold to individuals and families for **primarily noncommercial** purposes.
- **Credit**: limited line credit insurance.
- Any other line allowed by law or rule.

## Types of Licensees: Licensing Requirements

- A resident applicant files the uniform application and declares under penalty of refusal, suspension or revocation that the statements are true. The director approves only if the applicant:
  - is at least **18**
  - has committed no act listed in 1239(1)
  - has completed prelicensing education
  - has paid the fees
  - has passed the exam for each line

  [MCL 500.1205(1)]
- A resident individual must pass a **written exam** unless exempt under 1206b. No exam is given until the applicant completes a registered program of study. The completion certificate must be dated within **12 months** before the exam application. [MCL 500.1204(1),(2)]
- The exam or prelicensing education may be **waived** for an applicant who:
  - applies for a limited license
  - was a **licensed producer within the preceding 12 months**
  - holds a listed designation, such as **CPCU**, CLU, CIC, AAI, ChFC, CEBS, CFP, FLMI, LUTCF, RHU, REBC, HIA or **ARM**
  - holds an insurance-concentration associate, bachelor's or master's degree

  [MCL 500.1204(2)]
- The exam is entry level. It tests the line's subject matter, a producer's duties and responsibilities, and Michigan insurance law. [MCL 500.1204(3)]
- An applicant who fails, or does not appear, must **reapply and pay all exam fees again**. [MCL 500.1204(6)]
- Minimum prelicensing hours [MCL 500.1204a(1)(b)]:
  - property: **20**
  - casualty: **20**
  - personal lines: **20**
  - combined property and casualty producers and solicitors: **40** (also satisfies personal lines)
  - life: **20**
  - health: **20**
  - combined life and health: **40**
- Classroom, online and self-study hours may be combined. [MCL 500.1204a(2)]
- Every program must include instruction in **ethical practices** in marketing and selling insurance. The statute sets no hour count for this. [MCL 500.1204a(1)(c)]
- Statutory application fee: **$10**, nonrefundable. [MCL 500.240(1)(d)]

## Types of Licensees: Business Entity, Nonresident, Temporary

- **Business entity**: a corporation, association, partnership, LLC, LLP or other legal entity. [MCL 500.1201(d)]
- A business entity acting as a producer needs its own producer license. It must designate an **individual licensed producer responsible for compliance** with Michigan insurance law. [MCL 500.1205(2)]
- An entity's license may be suspended, revoked or refused if an individual licensee's violation was known, or should have been known, by a partner, officer or manager, and the violation was neither reported nor corrected. [MCL 500.1239(5)]
- **Nonresident** license requirements:
  - currently licensed and in good standing as a resident in the home state
  - application and fees submitted
  - the home state grants nonresident licenses to Michigan residents on the same basis (reciprocity)

  [MCL 500.1206a(1)]
- A producer who changes resident state files a change of address and a certification from the new state within **30 days**. No fee is charged. [MCL 500.1206a(3)]
- An applicant previously licensed for the same lines in another state needs **no prelicensing education or exam** if either:
  - still licensed there, or
  - applying within **90 days** of that license's cancellation, while in good standing

  [MCL 500.1206b(1)]
- A producer who **moves to Michigan** must apply for a resident license within **90 days** after establishing legal residence. No prelicensing education or exam is required for lines previously held. [MCL 500.1206b(2)]
- A nonresident who meets home-state CE requirements satisfies Michigan CE, if the home state reciprocates. [MCL 500.1204e(2)]
- **Temporary license**: up to **180 days**, with no exam. Available to:
  - the surviving spouse or personal representative of a producer who dies or becomes disabled
  - a member or employee of a business entity after its designated individual dies or becomes disabled
  - the designee of a producer entering active military service
  - others, in the public interest

  The director may require a sponsor. A temporary license ends when the business is disposed of. [MCL 500.1211b]

## Types of Licensees: Solicitor

- A solicitor is a natural person who solicits applications and collects premiums **on behalf of a licensed resident producer (agent)**. Three conditions apply:
  - the solicitor works under a **written contract** that specifies the solicitor's authority
  - the solicitor is licensed as a solicitor
  - the agent has notified the director of the contract

  [MCL 500.1211(1); MCL 500.1212]
- The solicitor applies with a notice of appointment from the sponsoring producer. The solicitor may act only after the license is received. [MCL 500.1214(1),(5)]
- A license for casualty solicitation also covers legal expense insurance. [MCL 500.1214(6)]
- A solicitor license continues until suspended, revoked or surrendered. The director demands surrender when the solicitor has no agent authority. [MCL 500.1214(7)]
- An agent must **immediately** notify the commissioner when a solicitor's authority is terminated, with full disclosure of any violations. [MCL 500.1218]
- A solicitor is a **fiduciary** for all money received. Failure to turn money over in a timely manner is prima facie evidence of a violation. [MCL 500.1216(1)]
- Solicitor license fee: **$10** per year. [MCL 500.240(1)(e)]

## Types of Licensees: Counselor

- A **counselor license** is required to do any of the following:
  - audit or abstract policies or annuities
  - give advice, counsel or opinion on a policy's benefits, coverage, terms, value or advantages
  - advertise or hold oneself out as an insurance counselor

  [MCL 500.1232]
- Exempt from the counselor license:
  - a licensed agent's customary advice
  - a person admitted to practice law in Michigan
  - use of the conferred designations "Certified Insurance Counselor" (initial capitals required), "accredited advisor in insurance", or "fraternal insurance counselor"

  [MCL 500.1232]
- An unlicensed person may not use terms such as "consultant" in a way that implies being a licensed counselor. [MCL 500.1232]
- The counselor applicant takes a written exam. The exam may be waived if the applicant was a licensed counselor within the preceding **12 months**. The commissioner decides within **60 days** after receiving a complete application. [MCL 500.1234(2)]
- Before providing services, the counselor and client both **sign a written agreement** that:
  - describes the work
  - states the fee
  - states that the **fee may not be waived under any circumstances**
  - discloses that the counselor receives a **commission** on any insurance placed as an agent

  The counselor keeps a copy for at least **2 years** after completing the services. [MCL 500.1236]
- Counselor license fee: **$10** per year. [MCL 500.240(1)(f)]

## Maintenance and Duration

- A producer license stays in effect, unless suspended or revoked, **as long as CE is completed by the due date**. There is no fixed expiration. [MCL 500.1206(2)]
- If a license lapses for a reason **other than CE**, the producer may reinstate it **without an exam** within **12 months** after the lapse. [MCL 500.1206(3)]
- **Change of legal name or address**: notify the commissioner within **30 days**. [MCL 500.1206(5)]
- Agents, solicitors, counselors and adjusters report a **mailing and email address** and must report any change within **30 days**. Notices of hearing may be served by first-class mail to the address on file. [MCL 500.1238(1),(2)]
- **Assumed name**: a producer using any name other than the legal name must notify the commissioner **before** using it. [MCL 500.1211a]
- **Reporting administrative actions**: a producer reports any administrative action from another jurisdiction, or from another Michigan agency, within **30 days after final disposition**, with a copy of the order. [MCL 500.1247(1)]
- **Reporting criminal prosecutions**: a producer reports any criminal prosecution in any jurisdiction within **30 days after the initial pretrial hearing date**, with the complaint and order. [MCL 500.1247(2)]

### Continuing Education

- CE hours are reviewed **every 2 years**. The director may stagger review dates. [MCL 500.1204c(1)]
- DIFS sets each producer's review date by birth month and year. The first review date leaves at least 12 months. [DIFS Insurance Licensee CE FAQ, updated 05/29/24]
- **24 hours** of approved CE are required each 2-year period. At least **3 hours must be ethics**. Classroom, home study and online courses all count. [MCL 500.1204c(2)]
- A CE **hour** is at least **50 minutes**. [MCL 500.1204c(18)(a)]
- **Carryover**: up to **12** excess hours may carry to the next period. **Ethics hours do not count toward the next period's ethics requirement**; DIFS counts extra ethics hours as general hours. Duplicate courses do not carry over. [MCL 500.1204c(13); DIFS CE Carryover FAQ]
- **90-day grace period**: if CE is not done by the license's expiration or review date, the producer has 90 days to finish.
  - During the grace period the producer may **not** sell or solicit new policies, bind coverage or otherwise act as a producer.
  - The producer **may** service existing policies and receive commissions on them.
  - If CE is still incomplete after 90 days, the director **cancels** the license, and the producer must reapply under 1204.

  [MCL 500.1204c(11)]
- DIFS shows this status as "Suspended for Education". [DIFS CE FAQ]
- A producer who has sold the business without meeting CE may service the old book and receive commissions, including partial commissions from the buyer. A producer still in the process of selling may do so for up to **12 months** after the review date. [MCL 500.1204c(12)]
- CE **waivers**:
  - required for military service or severe hardship
  - mandatory for producers licensed only for **travel or baggage** insurance whose employment is for another purpose
  - mandatory for producers licensed only for **limited line credit** insurance

  [MCL 500.1204c(9)]
- The provider files completion within **30 days**, with a **$1.00-per-hour** fee. [MCL 500.1204c(8)]
- **Long-term care**: a producer selling LTC needs a life or A&H license, a one-time **8-hour** LTC course, and **4 hours** of LTC training each 2-year CE period. [MCL 500.1204f(1),(5)]
- **Flood (NFIP)**: there is no separate Michigan statutory flood CE hour requirement. Producers selling NFIP policies must meet FEMA's minimum training under §207 of the Flood Insurance Reform Act of 2004. A DIFS-approved NFIP course earns **3 hours** of Michigan CE credit. [OFIS Bulletin 2007-07-INS]
- **New (2026 PA 78)**: for review dates after Dec 31, 2025, active participation in an approved professional insurance association may earn up to **4 hours** toward the 24. These hours do **not** count toward the 3 ethics hours. [MCL 500.1204c(14), am. 2026 PA 78, imd. eff. July 27, 2026]

## Disciplinary Actions

### Grounds (1239)

The director may place a license on probation, suspend or revoke it, or fine under 1244, for any 1239 cause.

- Under **1239(1)**, the director **shall not issue** a license for any of these causes [MCL 500.1239(1)]:
  - obtaining or attempting to obtain a license by misrepresentation or fraud
  - improperly withholding, misappropriating or converting money or property
  - intentionally misrepresenting the terms of an actual or proposed policy or application
  - a **felony conviction within 10 years** before the application
  - **regardless of date**, a felony involving violence or threat of violence (including domestic violence), criminal sexual conduct, or a fiduciary or financial crime (fraud, embezzlement, bribery, extortion)
  - having admitted or been found to have committed an unfair trade practice or fraud
  - fraudulent, coercive or dishonest practices, or incompetence, untrustworthiness or financial irresponsibility
  - **forging another's name** to an application or any document related to an insurance transaction
  - **knowingly accepting business from an unlicensed individual**
- Under **1239(2)**, the director **may refuse** to issue a license, and may discipline, for [MCL 500.1239(2)]:
  - incorrect, misleading, incomplete or materially untrue information on the application
  - other felonies
  - a license denied, suspended or revoked in another state
  - **using notes or reference material during the licensing exam**
  - violating insurance laws or the director's orders or subpoenas
  - failing to comply with a **child support** order
  - failing to pay Single Business Tax or Michigan Business Tax
- After a written denial, the applicant may make a written demand for a hearing within **30 days**. [MCL 500.1239(4)]
- The director may still act against a person under investigation even if the license has been surrendered or has lapsed. [MCL 500.1239(7)]

### Procedures

- **Solicitor, counselor or adjuster**: an applicant who is refused may request a hearing within **30 days** after the refusal notice is mailed. [MCL 500.1242(1)]
- **Summary suspension**: the director may suspend a license **without a prior hearing** when protecting the public requires emergency action. If requested, a hearing is held within **20 days**, and the decision comes within **30 days** after the hearing. [MCL 500.1242(4)]

### Fines and Cease and Desist

- **Chapter 12 (licensing) violations**, after a hearing, the director issues a **cease and desist order** and may also order [MCL 500.1244(1)]:
  - a civil fine of up to **$1,000 per violation**, or up to **$5,000 per violation** if the person **knew or reasonably should have known** of the violation; one order may not exceed **$50,000** in fines
  - a refund of overcharges
  - restitution
  - suspension or revocation
- **Knowingly violating a chapter 12 cease and desist order**: up to **$20,000 per violation**, with a **$100,000** cap per order, plus suspension or revocation. [MCL 500.1244(3)]
- **Unfair Trade Practices Act violations** (sections 2001-2050), after a hearing, bring a cease and desist order plus [MCL 500.2038(1)]:
  - up to **$1,000 per violation**, **$10,000 aggregate**; or, if the person **knew or reasonably should have known** of the violation, up to **$5,000 per violation**, **$50,000 aggregate** for all violations in a **6-month** period
  - suspension or revocation if the violations were **knowing and persistent**
  - refund of overcharges
- **Violating a UTPA cease and desist order**: up to **$20,000 per violation**, and/or suspension or revocation. [MCL 500.2040(1)]
- For a practice **not defined** in sections 2005-2025, the director may charge it as unfair after a hearing held at least **15 days** after the notice is served. The person gets **10 days'** preliminary notice and may ask for a trade conference. If the practice continues, the attorney general may seek an injunction in Ingham County circuit court. [MCL 500.2043]
- Before a hearing on a defined unfair practice, the person gets written notice and an opportunity to confer. The matter may be settled summarily by agreement. [MCL 500.2029]
- **Insurer** license: the director may suspend, revoke or refuse to renew it, after notice and hearing, when a chapter 13 violation makes the insurer's continued operation contrary to policyholders' or the public's interest. [MCL 500.1379]

## Unfair and Prohibited Trade Practices

- Sections 2001-2050 are the **Uniform Trade Practices Act**. Its purpose is to regulate insurance trade practices consistent with the McCarran-Ferguson Act (PL 15, 1945). [MCL 500.2001, .2002]
- "Person" includes producers, solicitors, counselors and adjusters. [MCL 500.2003(2)]
- **Withholding or falsifying rate information**: wilfully withholding, or knowingly giving false, information that affects rates carries a fine of up to **$300 per violation**, up to **$1,500 if wilful**, and **$10,000** maximum per order. [MCL 500.2474, .2478; MCL 500.2666, .2670]

### Misrepresentation

- Misrepresentation is any estimate, illustration, circular, statement, sales presentation or comparison that, by omitting or misstating a material fact, does any of the following [MCL 500.2005]:
  - misrepresents a policy's terms, benefits, advantages or conditions
  - misrepresents dividends
  - misrepresents an insurer's **financial condition**
  - uses a misleading policy name
  - **misrepresents to induce a lapse, forfeiture, exchange, conversion or surrender** (2005(f))
  - misrepresents a policy as a security
  - misrepresents guaranty-association coverage
- A policy name the commissioner has approved is **conclusively presumed** not misleading. [MCL 500.2005(e)]
- **Twisting**: knowingly making a misleading representation, or an incomplete or fraudulent comparison of policies, to induce someone to lapse, forfeit, surrender, terminate, retain, pledge, assign, borrow on or convert a policy, or to take a policy with another insurer. [MCL 500.2005a(a)]
- **High-pressure sales**: marketing that induces purchase by **force, fright, threat** or **undue pressure**. [MCL 500.2005a(b)]
- **Undisclosed solicitation**: marketing that fails to disclose conspicuously that its purpose is soliciting insurance and that an agent or insurer will make contact. [MCL 500.2005a(c)]
- **Section 2064**: no misrepresenting policy terms or benefits, estimating future dividends, or making a misrepresentation or **incomplete comparison** to induce a purchase or a lapse and replacement. [MCL 500.2064(1),(2)]
  - The commissioner **revokes** the license, and no new license is issued for **1 year**. [MCL 500.2064(3)]
  - It is a misdemeanor with a fine of up to **$2,000 per violation**, or county jail. [MCL 500.2069]
- Revocation under 2064 or 2066 requires written notice and a hearing at least **15 days** away. The revocation takes effect **10 days** after the order. An appeal to the supreme court must be taken within **10 days**. [MCL 500.2068]
- **Misrepresentation by applicants** voids a contract only if it is **material**, meaning knowing the facts would have led the insurer to refuse the contract. [MCL 500.2218(1)]

### False Information and Advertising

- It is an unfair practice to publish an advertisement, announcement or statement about the business of insurance, or about a person in it, that is **untrue, deceptive or misleading**, in any medium. [MCL 500.2007]
- Advertising may not conceal or misrepresent the **true identity of the insurer**. The name of the insurer assuming the risk must appear **more conspicuously** than a department's or general agency's name.
  - A fire-insurance representative may still advertise his or her own business.
  - Violation is a misdemeanor with a fine of up to **$500**.

  [MCL 500.2057]
- **Insurer financial condition**: a director or officer who falsely advertises it commits a misdemeanor, punishable by up to **$100** and/or **3 months**. An officer or agent who intentionally violates commits a misdemeanor, punishable by up to **$1,000** and/or **6 months**. [MCL 500.2055(1),(4)]
- No person may maintain an insurance office, or use an insurer's name, for a business not connected with insurance. [MCL 500.2059(1)]

### False Statement

- **False financial statement**: filing or circulating a false material statement of an insurer's financial condition, or making a false entry in (or omitting a true entry from) its books. [MCL 500.2014]
- **False application statements**: making false or fraudulent statements on or about an **application** to obtain a fee, commission, money or other benefit. [MCL 500.2018]
An intentional fraudulent report is cause for forfeiture of a domestic insurer's franchise, or for revocation of a foreign insurer's authority after a hearing. An officer or agent commits a misdemeanor punishable by up to $1,000 and/or 6 months. [MCL 500.2062]
- **Auditor duty**: an independent CPA must report in writing within **5 business days** to the board or audit committee if the insurer has materially misstated its financial condition. The insurer gives the commissioner a copy within **5 business days** of receipt. [MCL 500.1015(1)]

### Deception

- **Defamation of insurers**: making or circulating a statement that is **false, or maliciously critical of or derogatory to, the financial condition** of a person in the business of insurance, and calculated to injure that person. [MCL 500.2009]
- **Michigan libel and slander law**:
  - "Libel" includes radio and TV broadcasts.
  - Exemplary or punitive damages for libel require first giving notice to publish a **retraction**.
  - Public officials and public figures must prove by **clear and convincing** evidence that the falsehood was published knowingly or with reckless disregard.
  - Private individuals must show negligence, and recovery is limited to **economic damages, including attorney fees**.

  [MCL 600.2911(2),(6),(7),(8)]

### Boycott, Coercion and Intimidation

- It is an unfair practice to agree to commit, or by concerted action commit, any act of **boycott, coercion or intimidation** that results or tends to result in **unreasonable restraint of the business of insurance**. [MCL 500.2012]
- No one may sell insurance by intimidation or threats. No one may induce a purchase through a particular agent or insurer by promising to sell goods, lend money or provide services, or by threatening to refuse them. Section 2077(4) allows one exception. [MCL 500.1207(13)]
- A lender may not require, as a condition of credit, that the borrower buy insurance through a particular agent or insurer. The lender may reasonably approve or disapprove the insurer chosen, but may not reject a policy whose coverage exceeds the required minimum. Violation is a misdemeanor with a fine of up to **$100**. [MCL 500.2077(1),(3),(5)]
- The commissioner may refuse or deny a solicitor, adjuster or counselor license if the applicant's main business is likely to lead to coercion or indirect rebating. [MCL 500.1242(3)]

### Inducement

- It is unfair to issue or deliver stock, securities or other contracts **promising returns and profits as an inducement** to insurance. [MCL 500.2017]
- The rebating sections (2024, 2066, 2070) also prohibit inducements not specified in the policy. See Rebating below.

### Unfair Discrimination

- **Life and annuities**: no unfair discrimination between individuals of the **same class and equal expectation of life** in rates, dividends, benefits or terms. [MCL 500.2019]
- **Accident and health**: no unfair discrimination between individuals of the **same class and essentially the same hazard**. [MCL 500.2020]
- Refusing to insure, refusing to continue, or limiting coverage **because of** any of the following is prohibited [MCL 500.2027(a)(i)]:
  - race, color, creed
  - marital status (but marital status may be used to classify family units)
  - sex, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation
  - national origin
- **Residence, age, disability, lawful occupation or location** may be used only if reasonably related to the risk. [MCL 500.2027(a)(ii)]
- For **property** insurance, location may be used only if there is a **statistically significant relationship** between the location and the risk of **fire** loss within the area. "Area" means a **single ZIP code**. [MCL 500.2027(a)(iii)]
- It is unfair to refuse to insure, or to refuse to continue to insure, **solely because the applicant was previously denied coverage** by an insurer. [MCL 500.2027(b)]
- Different rates for the same coverage based on the listed traits are unfair unless the difference is actuarially sound and supported by loss statistics. [MCL 500.2027(c)]
- **Life insurers and race or color**: a life insurer may not discriminate by race or color. Violations bring:
  - for the insurer, a civil fine of **$1,000** per violation
  - for an officer or agent, a misdemeanor: up to **1 year** in jail and/or a fine of **$500-$1,000**

  [MCL 500.2082, am. 2025 PA 46, eff. Mar. 24, 2026]

### Rebating

- No insurer, agent or solicitor may offer, give or pay, directly or indirectly, any **rebate of premium**, part of the commission, or **valuable consideration or inducement not specified in the policy**, including stocks or securities. [MCL 500.2066(1)]
  - On satisfactory evidence of a violation, the commissioner **revokes** the license, and no license is issued for **1 year**. [MCL 500.2066(2)]
  - It is also a misdemeanor, with a fine of up to **$100 per violation** or county jail. [MCL 500.2069]
- **Insured who accepts a rebate**: an insured who knowingly accepts a rebate has the **insurance reduced** in proportion to the rebate's share of the total premium. The insured also commits a misdemeanor, with a fine of up to **$100**. [MCL 500.2070]
- **Allowed gift (P&C)**: an insurer or P&C producer may give an applicant or insured an article of merchandise costing the insurer **$50 or less per calendar year**. [MCL 500.2024b, am. 2018 PA 542]
- For comparison, a life insurer or agent may give a life applicant merchandise with an invoice value of **$5 or less**. [MCL 500.2024a]
- Section 2024 prohibits rebates and special inducements for life, annuity and A&H contracts. [MCL 500.2024]

### Claims and Other Practices (Course of Conduct)

- The unfair claims practices below count only when they show **a course of conduct indicating a persistent tendency**, not isolated incidents. [MCL 500.2026(1)]
  - misrepresenting facts or policy provisions
  - failing to acknowledge or act promptly on claim communications
  - refusing to pay without a reasonable investigation
  - failing to settle in good faith once liability is reasonably clear
  - compelling insureds to sue by lowballing
  - failing to explain a denial
- Insurers must keep a **complaint record** since the last examination. An agent who receives a written complaint about an insurer must **promptly forward it to the insurer**, unless the agent resolves it to the insured's satisfaction within a reasonable time. [MCL 500.2026(2)]
- **Timely claim payment**:
  - The insurer must specify what proof of loss it needs within **30 days** of receiving the claim.
  - A claim is timely if paid within **60 days** after proof of loss.
  - Late benefits to the insured (or a person directly entitled to benefits) bear **12% simple interest per year** from 60 days after proof of loss. A third-party tort claimant gets the 12% only if liability is not reasonably in dispute and a court has found that the insurer refused payment in bad faith.

  [MCL 500.2006(1),(3),(4)]
- **Workers' compensation**: a premium deposit may not exceed **the greater of 25% of projected annual premium or $2,500**, and the insurer may not require buying other insurance. [MCL 500.2016(1)(b),(c)]
- **Automatic credit insurance**: it is unfair to write insurance automatically on a debtor unless the debtor rejects it, unless the creditor pays the premium. [MCL 500.2023]
- **Rate information**: rating organizations and insurers must furnish rate information to an affected insured on written request. [MCL 500.2021]
- Violating chapter 5 (privacy) or its rules is itself an unfair trade practice. [MCL 500.2013]

## Fiduciary Responsibilities

- An agent is a **fiduciary** for all money received or held as agent. [MCL 500.1207(1)]
- Premiums and return premiums are **fiduciary money**. They must be **segregated** and **not commingled** with the agent's own money.
  - The agent may use **own money to open** the separate account.
  - Further deposits of own money are allowed **only to pay or avoid bank charges**, and must be separately accounted for.

  [MCL 500.1207(2)]
- The separate account must be at a **federally insured** state or federal institution, and **may bear interest**. [MCL 500.1207(3)]
- The agent may hold **return premiums** in the account to pay an insured's future premiums **with the insured's written authorization**. [MCL 500.1207(4)]
- Records must show at least the date received, payee name, amount and description of fiduciary money. They are open to examination by the director. [MCL 500.1207(6),(10)]
- **Failure to turn over fiduciary money in a timely manner is prima facie evidence** of a violation of fiduciary duty. [MCL 500.1207(8)]
- **Placing refused coverage**: if the agent's insurers refuse part of the coverage, the agent may place it through another licensed agent or a risk-sharing plan. The agent must tell the buyer **in writing** that the coverage is not in effect until the buyer receives **written evidence of insurance**. [MCL 500.1207(12)]
- An agent may not agree to pay losses out of commissions unless claims are adjusted by company adjusters or licensed independent adjusters. [MCL 500.1207(14)]

## Forgery

- **Forging another's name** to an insurance application, or to any document related to an insurance transaction, is a 1239(1)(h) cause. The director must deny the license and may suspend or revoke an existing one, and/or fine. [MCL 500.1239(1)(h)]

## Insurance Fraud Regulation

- A **fraudulent insurance act** is an act committed **knowingly and with intent to injure, defraud or deceive**. Examples [MCL 500.4503]:
  - presenting false material information in an **application**
  - presenting false material information in support of a **claim**, including computer-generated information
  - helping prepare such statements
  - soliciting or accepting risks for an **insolvent insurer**
  - removing or concealing an insurer's assets or records from the commissioner
  - diverting insurer funds
  - acting as or using a **runner, capper or steerer**
  - knowingly assisting or benefiting from the fraud
- **Runner, capper or steerer**: a person paid by a practitioner for procuring clients or patients in order to obtain insurance benefits or assert claims. A practitioner who advertises through public media is not one. [MCL 500.4501(h)]
- "Statement" includes proofs of loss, invoices, estimates, bills, claim forms, medical records and X-rays. [MCL 500.4501(i)]
- "Authorized agency" includes the state police, local police and sheriffs, prosecutors, federal investigators, DIFS (formerly OFIR) and the Department of State. [MCL 500.4501(a)]
- **Penalties** [MCL 500.4511]:
  - committing a fraudulent insurance act: a **felony**, up to **4 years** in prison and/or a fine of up to **$50,000**, plus mandatory **restitution**
  - **agreement or conspiracy** to commit one: a felony, up to **10 years** and/or **$50,000**, plus restitution
  - if a practitioner or insurer is found guilty, the court notifies the licensing authority
- **Sharing information**: on an authorized agency's **written request**, an insurer may release information about suspected fraud, such as applications, premium payment records, claim history and investigation materials. An insurer may also report suspected fraud on its own. Information may be used **only** to investigate suspected insurance fraud. [MCL 500.4507(1),(2),(6)]
- After an insurer reports, it may request in writing the agency's related information. The agency may provide it within **30 days** on a showing of good cause. [MCL 500.4507(3)]
- **Immunity**: a person who reports or furnishes information about suspected fraud **without malice** is not liable for libel, slander or other torts, unless the person knows the information is false. [MCL 500.4509(1),(3)]
- **Health care fraud conspiracy**: conspiring to defraud a health care corporation or health care insurer through a **false claim** for health care benefits is a **felony**, up to **10 years** and/or **$50,000**. [MCL 752.1005]
- **False death, sickness or disability claims**: an agent, physician, insured or other person who presents a false certificate or claim commits a **misdemeanor**, punishable by up to **$1,000** or up to **3 months** in county jail. [MCL 500.2088]

## Consumer Privacy Regulation (Chapter 5, Privacy of Financial Information)

### Scope and Definitions

- Chapter 5 protects **nonpublic personal financial information** about individuals who obtain insurance for **personal, family or household** purposes. It does **not** cover companies, or individuals buying for **business, commercial or agricultural** purposes. [MCL 500.501(1)]
- It does not override Michigan health-information confidentiality laws. [MCL 500.501(3)]
- **Consumer**: an individual who seeks, obtains or has obtained a personal-lines insurance product or service. Applicants are included. [MCL 500.503(f)]
- **Customer**: a consumer with a **continuing relationship** with the licensee. [MCL 500.503(h),(i)]
- **Licensee** includes licensed insurers and producers. [MCL 500.503(l)]
- **Nonpublic personal financial information** excludes health information protected by other law and publicly available information. [MCL 500.503(n)]
- **Opt out**: the consumer's direction not to disclose to **nonaffiliated third parties**. [MCL 500.503(o)]

### Notices

- A **producer** establishes a customer relationship when the consumer **obtains insurance through that licensee**. An insurer does so when it **delivers the policy**. [MCL 500.509(1)]
- The relationship ends if a policy is lapsed or inactive and there has been no contact for **12 consecutive months**. [MCL 500.509(2)(a)]
- **Initial notice**: a clear and conspicuous privacy notice must be given **no later than when the customer relationship is established**. It may come later if the relationship was not at the customer's election, or if earlier notice would substantially delay the transaction and the customer agrees. [MCL 500.507(1)(a); MCL 500.511(1)]
- **Annual notice**: required at least **once in any 12 consecutive months** while the relationship continues. [MCL 500.513(1)]
  - It is **not** required if the licensee shares information only under the 535/537/539 exceptions **and** its policies have not changed. [MCL 500.513(2), am. 2020 PA 90]
  - It is not required for **former customers**. [MCL 500.513(3)]
- **Notice content**: categories of information collected and disclosed, categories of recipients, the **right to opt out** and how to exercise it, and confidentiality and security practices. [MCL 500.515(1)]
- **Agent exemption**: an agent or employee of a principal licensee need not give notices, if the principal does and the agent discloses only to the principal or its affiliates. [MCL 500.505(1)]
- **Surplus lines**: surplus lines brokers comply by giving a **16-point** "PRIVACY NOTICE" statement and not disclosing information. [MCL 500.505(2)]
- **Delivery**: in writing, or electronically if the consumer agrees, by hand delivery, mail, or a website with required acknowledgment. [MCL 500.527(1)]
  - Posting a **sign in the office** or general advertising does **not** count.
  - Emailing a consumer who did not transact electronically does **not** count.
  - A notice may **never** be given **solely orally**.

  [MCL 500.527(2),(4)]

### Opt-Out

- Before disclosing to a **nonaffiliated third party**, the licensee must give an initial notice and an opt-out notice, and allow a **reasonable opportunity to opt out**. [MCL 500.529(1)]
- A **30-day** window from mailing (by mail form or toll-free number) is reasonable. [MCL 500.529(2)(a)]
- The licensee must honor an opt-out **as soon as reasonably practicable**. The consumer may opt out **at any time**. [MCL 500.523(1),(2)]
- An opt-out lasts until **revoked in writing**, or electronically if agreed. It survives the end of the relationship for information already collected, but not for a new relationship. [MCL 500.523(2),(3)]
- Joint consumers may receive one opt-out notice. One joint consumer may opt out for all. [MCL 500.521]
- Disclosing beyond what the initial notice describes requires a **revised notice** and a new opt-out opportunity. [MCL 500.525]
- **No opt-out right** applies in these cases:
  - **Service providers and joint marketing**: disclosure to a nonaffiliated service provider or joint marketer, if the licensee gave the initial notice and has a **contract** limiting use. [MCL 500.535]
  - **Transaction exceptions**: disclosures needed to effect, administer or enforce a transaction, including claims, servicing, reinsurance and the auto placement facility. [MCL 500.537]
  - **Other exceptions**: with consent, fraud prevention, to rating organizations, guaranty funds, auditors, regulators and law enforcement, to consumer reporting agencies under the FCRA, in a sale or merger, and to comply with law or subpoena. [MCL 500.539]
- **Account numbers**: a licensee may not give a policy, credit card or account number to a nonaffiliated third party for **telemarketing, direct mail or email marketing**. Exceptions: a consumer reporting agency, a service provider marketing the licensee's own products, a producer, affinity programs, or encrypted numbers without the key. [MCL 500.533]
- A licensee may not unfairly discriminate against a consumer who opts out. [MCL 500.543]
- A violation of chapter 5 or its rules is an **unfair trade practice** under the UTPA. [MCL 500.2013; Mich Admin Code R 500.560(a)]

### Safeguarding Rules (R 500.551-.560)

- Title: "Standards for Safeguarding Customer Financial Information". [Mich Admin Code R 500.551]
Chapter 5A took effect Jan. 20, 2021. MCL 500.555 requires a comprehensive written information security program; licensees had to implement it by Jan. 20, 2022, and the third-party service provider requirement in 555(6) by Jan. 20, 2023 (500.565(5)). Licensees with fewer than 25 employees, including independent contractors, are exempt from 555 (500.565(1)). So is an agent covered by its principal's program (500.565(3)), and a HIPAA-compliant licensee is exempt from all but 559 and 561 (500.565(2)). A licensee must notify the director within 10 business days of a qualifying cybersecurity event (500.559(1)). R 500.551-.560 remain in the Admin Code, but MCL 500.550 makes chapter 5A exclusive.
- The program must ensure the security and confidentiality of customer information, protect against anticipated threats, and protect against unauthorized access that could cause substantial harm or inconvenience. [R 500.554]
- Example steps that count as compliance [R 500.555-.559]:
  - assess risk
  - manage and control risk, including **staff training** and regular testing
  - oversee **service providers** with due diligence and contract requirements
  - adjust the program as technology, threats and business change
- Compliance with the **HIPAA** privacy and security rules for all customer information also satisfies chapter 5 and these rules. [R 500.560(b)]

## Flags

- **MCL 500.1204c amended by 2026 PA 78**, immediately effective **July 27, 2026**.
  - It adds association-participation CE credit of up to 4 hours (subsection 14).
  - Subsection 15 also says "any activity by an insurance producer, as determined by the director, may be credited". Its scope is unclear, and we found no DIFS implementation guidance.
  - The PSI outline (Sept 2021) predates this change. No questions test it.
- **MCL 500.2082** was amended by 2025 PA 46 (eff. Mar. 24, 2026). The current civil fine is $1,000 per violation for the insurer; for an officer or agent, up to 1 year in jail, a $500-$1,000 fine, or both. Older study materials may show different figures.
- **MCL 500.2027** was amended by 2023 PA 156 (eff. Feb. 13, 2024). It added gender, gender identity or expression, and sexual orientation. Older prep materials may omit these.
- **MCL 500.2024b**: the P&C gift limit is **$50 per calendar year** (2018 PA 542). Older materials may show the original 2005 limit, which we did not verify.
- **CE lapse wording**: the statute (1204c(11)) says a 90-day grace period, then the license is "cancelled". The DIFS FAQ calls it a 90-day "suspension", then "terminated for education", with reinstatement within 1 year of the review date by completing 24 hours and reapplying through NIPR. The questions use the statute's terms of service-only conduct and a 90-day window.
- **Solicitor prelicensing currency**: MCL 500.1214(2) says the solicitor's course must be completed within **6 months** before the application. MCL 500.1204(2), for producers, and the DIFS FAQ say **12 months**. This is a conflict for solicitors. The 6-month figure is not tested.
- **Ethics hours in prelicensing**: the statute requires ethics instruction but sets no hour count. A secondary source claims "6 hours of ethics and Michigan law", which we could not verify; the rule R 500.1-.6 PDF could not be downloaded. Not tested.
- **Statutory fees in MCL 500.240** (for example, the $10 exam fee) apply only when the director administers exams. PSI charges its own fee; see Exam Logistics.
- **Penalties outside the cited sections**: the 2069 misdemeanor penalties for 2064/2066 allow "imprisonment in the county jail" with no stated maximum term. Only the fine caps are tested.

# logistics: Exam Logistics

Sources: PSI Candidate Information Bulletin "Effective 6/10/2025". The DIFS Examination Information page links to it as PSI bulletin 3560; the PDF was regenerated July 6, 2026 and its text is identical to the 6/10/2025 version. Also used: DIFS web pages fetched October 12, 2026.

- **Exam provider**: PSI Services LLC, under contract with DIFS. [PSI CIB eff. 6/10/2025, p.2]
- **Registration**: online at test-takers.psiexams.com/midifs, where the first and last name must match the government ID exactly, or by phone at **855-579-4639**. [PSI CIB p.3-5; DIFS Examination Information page]
- **Exam fee**: **$41**, not refundable or transferable. The fee is valid for **one year** from payment. [PSI CIB p.3]
- **Extra time**: time-and-a-half costs an extra **$50**. ADA accommodations carry no extra fee. Both must be scheduled by phone. [PSI CIB p.5]
- **Remote testing**: discontinued. DIFS stopped accepting new remote registrations **June 10, 2025**. Exams are now **in person only** at PSI test centers. Remote exams already scheduled had to be taken by June 30, 2025, or rescheduled in person at no fee. [DIFS "Remote Examination Discontinuance", June 9, 2025; PSI CIB p.1]
- **Locations**: Michigan PSI sites include Dearborn, Holt-Lansing, Southfield (two sites), Grand Rapids, Gaylord and Marquette. PSI centers in other states may also be used. [PSI CIB p.6]
- **Rescheduling and no-shows**: cancel or reschedule at least **2 days** before the exam, or the fee is forfeited. Voicemail and email are not acceptable. The fee is also forfeited for:
  - not appearing
  - not being checked in by the start time
  - lacking proper ID

  [PSI CIB p.5]
- **Arrival**: arrive **30 minutes** early. [PSI CIB p.6]
- **ID**: one valid, unexpired, government-issued ID with **photo and signature**, matching the registered name. Accepted IDs:
  - state driver's license or state ID card
  - US passport
  - US military ID
  - alien registration card
  - Canadian government ID

  [PSI CIB p.6-7]
- **P&C Producer/Solicitor (16-81) format**: **150 scored items**, **2.5 hours**, cut score **74%**. [PSI CIB p.9; DIFS Insurance Examination Cut Scores PDF]
  - DIFS lists the 74% cut score as effective **5/1/2020**. Items were updated effective **July 2, 2020** for the 2019-2020 no-fault reforms. [DIFS Ins_Exam_Cut_Scores.pdf]
  - Up to about 10% unscored experimental items (generally 1-10) may be added. They count against exam time. [PSI CIB p.8]
  - The tutorial, up to 15 minutes, does not count toward exam time. [PSI CIB p.8]
- **Prelicensing education is required** for 16-81. The program is **40 hours** for the combined property and casualty producer/solicitor course; property-only, casualty-only or personal lines programs are 20 hours each. [MCL 500.1204a(1)(b); DIFS Pre-licensing Education FAQ; PSI CIB p.2]
  - Courses may be classroom or self-study. [DIFS PE FAQ]
  - Ethics instruction must be included, with no specific hour count in statute. [MCL 500.1204a(1)(c)]
  - Exam results are **invalidated** if prelicensing education was not completed beforehand. [PSI CIB p.2]
- **Prelicensing certificate**: valid **12 months** from completion. The exam must be passed within that window, or the coursework must be repeated. The provider reports completion electronically; do not bring the certificate to the test site. [DIFS PE FAQ; DIFS "How to become licensed as a Resident Producer"; PSI CIB p.2]
- **Prelicensing waiver**: available with designations such as CPCU, CIC, AAI or ARM (for P&C), or an insurance-concentration degree. The waiver must be **requested in writing and granted before scheduling** the exam. [DIFS PE FAQ; MCL 500.1204(2)]
- **Score validity**: passing results are valid **12 months** from the pass date. [PSI CIB p.2; NIPR Michigan state page]
- **License application**:
  - It is filed electronically through **NIPR** (Electronic Resident Licensing). [PSI CIB p.2; DIFS resident producer page]
  - Fees for a resident **producer** license are a **$10 application fee plus a $5 NIPR transaction fee**. A resident **solicitor** pays **$20** (the $10 application fee plus the $10 solicitor license fee) plus the $5 transaction fee. [DIFS resident producer page; DIFS "How to Become Licensed as a Solicitor"; MCL 500.240(1)(d),(e)]
  - An application is valid **180 days** from entry into the DIFS database. If the exam is not passed within 180 days, a new application and fee are required. [DIFS resident producer page]
- **Retakes**: a new appointment cannot be made the same day. A candidate who fails on Wednesday can call Thursday and retest as soon as Friday, if space allows. There is no mandatory waiting period beyond that, but each attempt requires a new registration and fee. [PSI CIB p.5; MCL 500.1204(6)]
  - Retaking an exam already passed within the past year requires DIFS permission. [PSI CIB p.5]
- **Background check and fingerprints**: DIFS states that it "performs several background checks on each applicant"; for agencies, the checks cover the designated responsible licensed producer, owners, partners, officers and directors. Neither DIFS's resident-producer licensing page nor the PSI bulletin lists a **fingerprint** requirement. See Flags. [DIFS "Insurance Producer Application Background Questions"]
- **Content outline**: the P&C 16-81 outline that PSI links today ("PropCas Prod 16-81x.pdf" on proctor2.psionline.com) is **byte-identical** to the copy this guide is based on. The PDF's creation date is **Sept 2, 2021**, so **no revision after Sept 2021** was found as of October 12, 2026. [PSI outline PDF metadata; PSI CIB p.9 link]
- **Contacts**: DIFS Licensing at 877-999-6442 or difs-licensing@michigan.gov. [PSI CIB p.2]

## Flags

- **Fingerprints**: not verified either way. Official DIFS pages and the PSI bulletin are silent. Third-party prep sites conflict: Achievable says fingerprints are required; Aceable says they are not. Confirm with DIFS at 877-999-6442 before relying on either.
- **Time to apply after passing**: PSI says passing scores are valid 12 months, while DIFS says a NIPR application is valid 180 days, and the exam must be passed within that period. In practice, file the NIPR application, then test within 180 days, and keep the pass within its 12-month validity.
- **PSI fee and bulletin**: the $41 fee and the bulletin dated 6/10/2025 are what DIFS linked as of 10/12/2026. PSI states that fees, cut scores and outlines are subject to change.
