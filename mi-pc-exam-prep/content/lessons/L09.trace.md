- **Michigan versus generic.** Every Michigan rule is said as Michigan law: "In Michigan, every fire policy" (scene 1),
  "In Michigan, if the sides disagree" (3), "Michigan law: ..." in the scene 4 fail and win texts, "In Michigan, 1 year
  after the loss" (5), "Michigan's Essential Insurance Act" (6), "the Act" (7, 8), "under Michigan's Essential Insurance
  Act", "the Act prohibits it" and "Outside the Act, Michigan lets..." (10), "Michigan's general casualty minimum" (7 reveal), "Michigan law: ..." in the scene
  12 fail and win texts and "In Michigan, a home and auto insurer..." (12 reveal), "the Michigan Property and Casualty
  Guaranty Association" (13), "Michigan's $5 million guaranty claim cap" (14). TRIA is federal (scene 11 says "a federal
  law").
- **Redlining scope (scene 10, rows 54-55a).** Every statement that location may not be used is scoped to the Essential
  Insurance Act and home (plain: home or auto) coverage. Since the cross-lesson audit (X12), beat 1 also states the general
  rule for property insurance outside the Act (reg_facts.md:339, MCL 500.2027(a)(iii); question reg-045): location may be
  used only with a statistically significant fire-loss link in the ZIP code. "Outside the Act" is the only interaction
  claimed, and it rests on PF:106 (chapter 21 governs home and auto where the Code conflicts) and PF:184 (home insurance
  excludes business property). The lesson does not say whether 2027(a)(iii) also reaches home insurance in some other way.
  So "never its location" (beat 0), "the Act prohibits it" and recap item 4 ("Act: ... not age or location") no longer read
  as clashing with reg-045, whose answer uses the ZIP-code wording that beat 1 now gives in its own, scoped place.
- **Scene 7 framing (row 36).** As in the old review (change 1), the termination timing is asked as what the Essential
  Insurance Act allows ("Fastest the Act allows?"). The win text adds "A policy may give more", because PF:828 (FC&S summary
  of the unverified HO 01 21) says the Michigan homeowners endorsement may promise 30 days. The 30-day option is a FAIL only
  because the question asks for the fastest; its outcome says it is legal (row 41).
- **Reasons in the first 55 days.** The lesson does not say whether a first-55-days notice must list each reason (old
  review: not settled by the text). "Each specific reason" is tied only to the 30-day rule (scene 6 beat 1, sign 1), and the
  scene 7 phone-call FAIL rests only on the missing written notice.
- **Scene 4 option 1.** The FAIL is "the insurer's appraiser picks alone": the statute has the two appraisers choose an
  impartial umpire together, or a circuit judge when they cannot agree within 15 days (PF:90). The narration does not claim
  what a court would do with such an umpire.

  Millers' own policy period. The Millers' payment is not promised within 30 days, because the withholding exception
  (PF:83, 85) could apply to a large fire loss; the rule is stated with its exception, and the exception's plain meaning is
  on screen. The withholding percentages and caps are not taught (same choice as the old lesson).
- **Scene 8 (row 45).** "Eligible person" now says what eligibility gets you, using the operating rule in PF:180 and
  PF:211. It is not presented as the Act's purpose (PF:309 flag). The swipe's "Not eligible" therefore means "outside that

- **Scene 12.** The terminating insurer is the Millers' insurer, so renewing the Millers and canceling their policy are
