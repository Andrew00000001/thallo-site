## Added in the new lesson (all sourced; see L11.trace.md)

- Named perils vs open perils as a term card, with the party guest-list analogy (now also in the drawing's row headings),
  a "?" for a cause the policy never mentions (shut out by named perils, let in by open perils) and the "exclusions still
  apply" trap (trace rows 5-11; cp_personal-06, mn_prop-10).
- HO-2 vs HO-3 vs HO-5 practice cards from the confusion-pair drills and prop-gap-09: the paint spill and the ice-and-snow
  roof (HO-2), the lost ring that HO-3 doesn't pay and HO-5 can (2000 edition) (trace rows 12, 20-24; cp_personal-10).
- Rosa's choice: HO-3 is not available to a tenant, HO-6 is for condo unit owners like her grandma, HO-4 is the tenant's
  form (trace rows 28, 33-34; PF:495-497, 519).
- Coverage C away from home: a bike stolen outside Jen's office is covered (trace row 39; PF:510, 564, 682).
- The "Which coverage pays?" sort: attached garage and repair lumber (A), shed and detached garage (B), stolen TV and
  smoke-stained couch (C) (trace rows 35-36, 42-44; prop-gap-12, prop-gap-13, mn_prop-14). The rule behind the lumber item
  (Coverage A also covers repair materials on site) is spoken and labeled in scene 6 beat 0 before the sort (trace row 42).
- Additional living expense explained with hotel and restaurant bills above normal costs; civil authority shown as a fire
  next door and a city order (trace rows 46, 48; cp_personal-07, prop-044).
- The burglary choice adds the $200 money limit to the $1,500 jewelry theft limit ($1,700) and shows that theft is a
  covered peril (trace rows 59-63).
- The tree choice adds the "wind-blown trees get nothing" mistake: removal of a windstorm tree blocking the driveway is
  covered (trace rows 71-75; mn_prop-17).
