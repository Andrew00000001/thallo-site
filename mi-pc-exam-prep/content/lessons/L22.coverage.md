## Added in v2 (all sourced; see the v2 trace)
- Going uninsured is a misdemeanor, and an uninsured employer can be sued (s2 options[0]): MCL 418.641(1)-(2). The s5 example and beats[1] tie the exclusive remedy to an employer that carries comp, so the two rules don't clash.
- An employee's agreement to waive comp isn't valid, except certain after-injury elections (s2 options[1]): MCL 418.815.
- Paying claims from operating funds without state approval doesn't secure comp (s2 options[2]): MCL 418.611(1); core-wcg-23.
- The employee's own ordinary carelessness doesn't bar comp (s3 beats[2], s4 items[2]): MCL 418.301(1), 418.305.
- The court decides whether an intentional tort occurred (s6 options[0]): MCL 418.131(1).
- An example of the 80% benefit: $1,000 a week after taxes pays $800 (s7 drawing label): core_facts Benefits example.
- FELA's full name, the Federal Employers' Liability Act (s9 label): mn_core-28; core-wcg-07.
- Part Two pays only where the law allows a suit, like a third-party-over suit, and not for injury the employer causes on purpose; Part Four includes providing immediate medical care; the audit is of actual exposure, usually payroll (s11 spots): Part Two A and C; cp_commercial-15.
