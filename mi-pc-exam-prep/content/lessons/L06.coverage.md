## Added in v2 (all sourced; see the trace)
- Insurable interest usually comes from property rights, a contract or potential legal liability (CF 214), scene 1 `plain`;
  spoken in beat 0 as "ownership or other property rights, from a contract, or from potential legal liability".
- A policy can't pass to a new owner without the insurer's written consent (CF 108, CF 401), scene 2 option 2 win text.
- The loss ratio is watched across the insurer's book of business (Q core-022), and the expense ratio is underwriting expenses
  divided by premium (CF 221), scene 3.
- A physical, moral or morale swipe with five cards from cp-cp_personal-01 drills and Q core-023, scene 5.
- Hazard vs peril as a choice, with the oily rags (Q core-bas-03), scene 6.
- The full IRMI wording for each construction class, and "brick veneer still counts as frame", scene 7 spots.
- A plain-English meaning of coinsurance (can cut a payment on underinsured property, CF 363), scene 9 beat 2.
- A sixth direct-or-indirect example set (heat-warped shelves, smoke, fire-hose water, lost catering orders, lost rent, extra
  costs) from cp-cp_personal-07 and Q core-031, scene 11.
- The broad evidence rule weighs all relevant evidence of value (mn-mn_core-16 unpack), scene 12 beat 2.
- The stated amount payment is figured before any deductible (CF 505), scene 14 option 1 win text and label.
