## Notes for the verifier

- Every amount is the 05 11 edition: named in the title subtitle, on screen in each `src`, spoken in scene 0 ("the twenty
  eleven H O three"), and spoken again where an HO amount or period is edition-specific (scene 10 reveal: "Under the twenty
  eleven form ... two thousand five hundred dollars"; scene 11: "the twenty eleven form says sixty days", "two years under
  the twenty eleven form"). The worked example and slider ignore the deductible: spoken in scene 9 ("Ignore the deductible
  here") and shown in the slider panel ("no deductible shown"). Scene 10 continues the same example.
- Row 42: the lesson states only the court rule (PF:808, 810) and that none of the Millers' January losses was a fire. It does not
  use PF:816 (FC&S's summary of HO 01 21, flagged because the endorsement text was not seen). The "not even Jen" outcome rests
  on the unendorsed Condition R (row 41, prop-067), which the Michigan court rule narrows only for fire.
- Row 40: the fraud premise was changed from a power-surge TV (not a paid claim: the surge peril excludes the electronics of
  home entertainment units, mn-mn_prop-15 watch_out) to the couch under Tuesday's cave-in (weight of ice, snow or sleet, no
  fine print in the sources), so the story never implies that a false claim would be a covered loss when it would not.
- Rows 8-10: the freezing rule is stated as the form's condition ("freezing needs reasonable care: heat on, or water shut off
  and drained"), which applies to the house (PF:667) and to the freezing named peril for belongings (mn-mn_prop-15 watch_out).
  The outcome narration says "the freezing damage isn't covered" without naming a coverage.
- Row 13: sort items[0] and [2] name the water damage ("soaks the laundry room", "soaks the drywall"), and the why texts say
  the water damage is covered, not the hose; this follows mn-mn_prop-15's watch_out.
- Story outcomes and why they are clean cases: the 70-day vacancy is stated as "consecutive" and the policy as the 2011 HO-3;
  the trip leaves the heat off (stated in the set-up), and each option either does nothing, shuts off without draining, or
  shuts off and drains; the $20,000 roof loss is above both small-loss tests; "next month" is inside 180 days and "a year
  later" is past it; the couch lie is an intentional false statement about a loss that would have been covered.
- The slider has no `goal`: with the goal line the player had to shrink the panel to 0.82em (lshot warning). The challenge
  is posed in narration (scene 9 beat 5), and the panel itself shows "Coverage A $200,000 meets it: full replacement cost"
  and "Insurer pays $20,000" when the learner reaches it; reveal 0 states the answer.
- Not taught (left out on purpose, as in the old lesson): the power-surge peril's electronics exclusion, peril fine print for
  falling objects and windstorm, Section I Exclusions B, appraisal, the mortgage clause, the 80% calculation exclusions.
  Nothing in the lesson says a surge damaged electronics or that a TV surge loss would be covered.
