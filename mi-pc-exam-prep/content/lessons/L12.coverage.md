## Added in the new lesson (all sourced; see L12.trace.md)

- Open perils vs named perils as a term card, with a party analogy (guest list vs banned list) (trace rows 2-4).
- Freezing as a three-way decision: do nothing, shut off without draining, shut off and drain (trace rows 8-10).
- Vacant vs unoccupied trap, tied to the Millers' wedding trip (trace row 18, confusion pair cp_personal-08).
- ACV defined in plain English (trace row 26, confusion pair cp_personal-05).
- Plain meanings for earth movement, neglect, intentional loss and governmental action (trace row 21, mn_prop-22).
- Accidental discharge pays the water damage, not the appliance it escaped from (trace row 13, mn_prop-15 watch_out).
- A claim-clock sort that practises the 60-day, 30-day and 2-year rules (scene 12; trace rows 35-38).

## Dropped

Nothing the old lesson taught was dropped. Three wording-level changes: the old example list "fire, windstorm, theft and
vandalism" for the basic perils is now "fire and windstorm" (examples, not a fact list); the old quiz's $300,000 numbers
were replaced by the slider challenge that tests the same rule and trap (row 22); and the proof-of-loss and suit rules are
practised in the scene 12 sort rather than repeated in the recap (the recap's five items are taken by the perils, freezing
and flood, vacancy, the 80% rule, and fraud with the Michigan payment rule).
