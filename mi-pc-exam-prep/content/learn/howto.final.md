## 1. The techniques that work best

**Retrieval practice.** Pull answers out of memory instead of rereading. In one study, students who took recall tests on a prose passage remembered more of it 2 days and 1 week later than students who reread it, even though rereading made them more confident. Rereading came out ahead only on a test given 5 minutes later [Roediger & Karpicke 2006].
*In the app:* Practice, Flashcards, Math lab, Mock exam, all the Games (Lightning round, Survival, Match, Boss battles) and the quick checks inside each lesson on the **Watch** tab, which pause the lesson until you answer. In Practice, turn on **Recall first (hide choices)** to answer from memory before you see the options.

**Spaced repetition.** Review material in sessions spread over days instead of in one block. A review of 317 experiments found that spaced study consistently beat massed study, and that the best gap between study sessions grew as the test got further away [Cepeda et al. 2006].
*In the app:* **Start today's session** on the Today tab brings questions back when they are due. Questions you miss come back in your next session, and questions you get right come back after gaps that grow. Streaks, XP and badges are there to help you show up every day.

**Interleaving.** Mix topics within a practice set instead of doing one topic at a time. In one small study of 18 college students learning volume formulas, the students who practiced mixed problems scored lower during practice (60% vs. 89%) but far higher on a test a week later (63% vs. 20%) [Rohrer & Taylor 2007]. A major review rated interleaving moderately useful, because its benefits had only begun to be studied systematically [Dunlosky et al. 2013].
*In the app:* in Practice, choose **All sections (weighted like the exam)**. Start today's session also mixes sections. Under Memory aids, **Confusion pairs** drill look-alike ideas side by side.

**Elaboration ("explain why").** Ask why a fact is true and how it connects to what you already know. The same review rated these "explain why" methods (elaborative interrogation and self-explanation) moderately useful. They show promise, but the evidence for them is more limited than for testing and spacing [Dunlosky et al. 2013].
*In the app:* when the feedback appears, say why the right answer is right and why your pick was wrong, then read the explanation. The Learn tab has the full rules, with sources.

**Memory aids.** Memory hooks are short cues, such as acronyms, rhymes and images, for facts you must memorize word for word, like lists and numbers. The evidence for memory tricks is thinner. The same review covered two of them, the keyword mnemonic and forming mental images while reading, and rated both low utility. The keyword mnemonic helped only with limited kinds of material and over short retention intervals [Dunlosky et al. 2013].
*In the app:* **Memory hooks** appear after related answers and under Memory aids, always listing the exact facts they stand for. **Picture summaries** put related rules in one table, step list or timeline, and **Key numbers** lists limits, deadlines and amounts. Use hooks for lists and numbers, not as a substitute for understanding the rule.

**Confidence ratings.** Rate how sure you are before you see the answer. In one study, errors made with high confidence were more likely to be corrected after feedback. They were also more likely to come back a week later when the correct answer had been forgotten [Butler et al. 2011].
*In the app:* keep **Rate how sure I am** turned on in Practice. Missed questions you marked Sure come back first in Start today's session. A right answer you marked Unsure or Guessed doesn't earn a longer gap, and a lucky guess comes back the next day.

## 2. A study plan

The exam has 150 scored items. You need 111 correct (74%) to pass, so you can miss no more than 39 [PSI outline].

Each day, do Start today's session first, then new material, roughly by weight. For each new block, read it in Learn, then practice that section. A full Mock exam takes 2.5 hours, so put it on a day when you have the time.

**6+ weeks (45-60 min/day).** One block a week: General and Basics; Homeowners and Dwelling; Auto; CPP and BOP; Workers' Comp, Other and Regulation. Then do mixed practice, Confusion pairs, two or more full Mock exams, and work on your weak areas.

**4 weeks (60-90 min/day).** Weeks 1-3: General, Basics and Regulation; Homeowners, Dwelling and Auto; CPP, BOP, Workers' Comp and Other. Week 4: mixed practice, one or two full Mock exams, and weak areas.

**2 weeks (90-120 min/day).** Days 1-11: Basics and General (3 days), Regulation (1), Homeowners and Dwelling (2), Auto (2), CPP and BOP (2), Workers' Comp and Other (1). Days 12-13: mixed practice and one full Mock exam. Day 14: light review and rest.

Don't skip the small areas: BOP, Workers' Comp, Other and Dwelling add up to 27 items [PSI outline].

## 3. Test-day strategy

- **Pace.** You get 2.5 hours for 150 scored items, and passing is 111 correct (74%) [PSI outline]. A small number of unscored experimental items may be added: the bulletin says one to ten, generally about 10% of the number of questions. These items also use up exam time [PSI bulletin]. The bulletin doesn't say they are marked, so treat every item as scored. Plan for up to about 165 items at about 50 seconds each, which leaves about 12 minutes to go back. The screen shows the minutes remaining [PSI bulletin]. Aim to finish about 60 items by the time it shows 100 minutes left, and about 120 by the time it shows 50 minutes left.
- **Use the tutorial.** The on-screen tutorial (up to 15 minutes) doesn't count toward your exam time. Its sample questions let you practice answering and reviewing your answers [PSI bulletin].
- **Read the stem's last line first** to see what is being asked.
- **Use only the facts given.** PSI's Ohio insurance bulletin tells candidates to "only consider the actual information given in the question" and not to read in possibilities or exceptions [PSI Ohio bulletin]. The Michigan bulletin has no tips list, but the advice fits.
- **Watch for EXCEPT and NOT.** In an "all of the following EXCEPT" item, three choices fit the stem. Pick the one that doesn't.
- **Eliminate** clearly wrong choices first.
- **Guess, note, return.** If you're stuck, pick your best answer, write the question number on your scratch paper and move on. You can go back to any question and change your answer as long as exam time hasn't run out [PSI bulletin]. The bulletin doesn't mention a flag button, so use your scratch paper to track questions. (The **Flag for review** button in the app's Mock exam is only for practice.) Never leave an item blank. Passing depends on how many you get right, and a blank can't be right [PSI outline].
- **Logistics.** Arrive 30 minutes before your start time. If you aren't checked in by your start time, you can't test and you lose your fee [PSI bulletin]. Bring one valid, unexpired, government-issued ID with your photo and signature, such as a state driver's license or a US passport. The first and last name on it must match the name you registered under [PSI bulletin]. Phones, electronic watches, handheld calculators, reference materials, food and drinks aren't allowed. You get a piece of scratch paper and a pencil [PSI bulletin]. Practice Math lab problems by hand. You may leave the room only to use the restroom, with the proctor's permission, and you get no extra time [PSI bulletin].

## 4. Sleep, spacing and focus

- **Sleep.** Adults should regularly sleep 7 or more hours a night. Getting less is linked to impaired performance and more errors [Watson et al. 2015]. Sleep helps you retain what you learned [Rasch & Born 2013], so don't trade it for late-night cramming.
- **Spread it out.** Spreading your study across different days beats cramming it into a single day [Cepeda et al. 2006].
- **Focus.** People are slower right after switching tasks, and they usually make more errors [Monsell 2003]. Silence your phone and do one thing at a time.
