# gen: General Insurance

Outline weight: 10.5% (16 items). Concepts below are national and tested in generic terms; Michigan-specific twists are noted where they exist.

## Risk Management Key Terms
- **Risk**: uncertainty about whether given events will happen. In practice the word also means the insured person or property. [IRMI glossary "risk"]
- **Pure vs. speculative risk**: pure risk can only cause a loss, never a gain, and is generally insurable. Speculative risk can cause a gain or a loss (a business venture, gambling) and generally is not insurable. [IRMI "pure risk"; "speculative risk"]
- **Exposure**: being subject to loss from some hazard or contingency. The word also means the rating unit or premium base (for example, payroll per $100). [IRMI "exposure"]
- **Hazard**: a condition that increases the probability of loss, e.g., poor housekeeping in a factory or inadequate lighting in a crime-prone area. [IRMI "hazard"]
- **Peril**: a cause of loss, e.g., fire, windstorm, collision. [IRMI "peril"]
- **Loss**: the reduction in value the policy responds to. A direct loss comes from direct damage to property. An indirect (consequential) loss follows from that damage, e.g., loss of rent. [IRMI "direct loss"; "consequential loss"]

## Methods of Handling Risk
- **Avoidance**: preventing the risk entirely by not doing the activity, e.g., a contractor declines remediation jobs. [IRMI "avoidance"]
- **Retention**: planned acceptance of losses through deductibles, deliberate noninsurance, or loss-sensitive plans. Self-insurance is a form of retention. [IRMI "risk retention"]
- **Sharing**: each group member's premiums and losses are allocated within the group by a set formula. Insurance pools and reciprocals work this way: subscribers pool their risks. [IRMI "risk sharing"; "reciprocal"]
- **Reduction (loss control)**: lowering the frequency of losses (prevention, e.g., driver training) or their severity (reduction, e.g., sprinklers, firewalls). [IRMI "loss control"; "loss reduction"]
- **Transfer**: shifting the financial consequences of a risk to another party. The two main forms are buying insurance and noninsurance contractual transfer, such as a hold-harmless or indemnity agreement. [IRMI "hold harmless agreement"]

## Elements of an (Ideally) Insurable Risk
- There are six characteristics:
  1. A large number of exposure units.
  2. Accidental and unintentional loss.
  3. A determinable and measurable loss.
  4. No catastrophic loss.
  5. A calculable chance of loss.
  6. An economically feasible premium.

  [Rejda & McNamara, *Principles of Risk Management and Insurance*, 13th ed., "Characteristics of an Ideally Insurable Risk"]

## Adverse Selection & Law of Large Numbers
- **Adverse selection**: an imbalance in an exposure group. It occurs because people who see a high chance of loss for themselves buy insurance far more often than people who see a low chance. Underwriting exists largely to counter it. [IRMI "adverse selection"]
- **Law of large numbers**: the larger the number of exposure units independently exposed to loss, the more likely actual losses will equal expected losses. In other words, a larger pool makes the data more credible. [IRMI "law of large numbers"]

## Reinsurance
- **Reinsurance**: the reinsurer, for a premium, indemnifies the "reinsured" (the primary insurer, also called the ceding company) for part or all of the liability it assumed. [IRMI "reinsurance"; "ceding company"]
- **Treaty vs. facultative**:
  - Treaty: an agreement covering whole classes of business. The reinsurer must accept every risk that qualifies under the treaty. [IRMI "treaty reinsurance"]
  - Facultative: each risk is offered and negotiated separately, and the reinsurer may decline any submission. [IRMI "facultative reinsurance"]
- **Pro rata (proportional) vs. excess of loss (nonproportional)**:
  - Pro rata: the reinsurer receives a set percentage of the premium and pays the same percentage of losses. Quota share is a pro rata form that cedes an agreed percentage of every risk in a class. Surplus reinsurance covers amounts above the ceding company's retention, and losses are shared in proportion to each party's share of the limit. [IRMI "pro rata reinsurance"; "proportional reinsurance"; "quota share reinsurance"; "surplus reinsurance"]
  - Excess of loss: the reinsurer pays the part of a loss above the ceding company's retention, up to a limit. Its premium is calculated independently of the original premium. [IRMI "excess of loss reinsurance"; "nonproportional reinsurance"]
- **Purposes**:
  - Capacity: the largest amount of insurance a company can write. [IRMI "capacity"]
  - Surplus relief: offsetting unusual drains on surplus, especially when premium volume is growing fast. [IRMI "surplus relief"]
  - Stabilizing results and protecting against catastrophes. [IRMI "reinsurance"; "capacity"]
- If an insurer reinsures part of a risk and the reinsurer pays late, the original insurer still owes the Michigan timely-payment interest. [MCL 500.2006(5)]

## Types of Insurers
- **Stock company**: owned by stockholders, with a capital fund they paid in on top of surplus and reserves. [IRMI "stock company"]
- **Mutual company**: owned by its insureds. Every owner is an insured and every insured is an owner. [IRMI "mutual company"]
- **Reciprocal (interinsurance exchange)**: an unincorporated group of subscribers who pool risks. It is managed by an attorney-in-fact. [IRMI "reciprocal"]
- **Lloyd's**: an association and marketplace of independent underwriters, not an insurance company. [IRMI "Lloyd's of London"]
- **Fraternal benefit society**: members share a common ethnic, religious, or vocational affiliation. Fraternals mainly write life insurance. [IRMI "fraternal benefit society"]

## Financial Status: Independent Rating Services
- **A.M. Best Financial Strength Rating (FSR)**: an independent opinion of an insurer's ability to meet its ongoing policy and contract obligations. [A.M. Best, *Guide to Best's Financial Strength Ratings*]
- **A.M. Best scale**:

  | Rating | Descriptor |
  |---|---|
  | A++, A+ | Superior |
  | A, A- | Excellent |
  | B++, B+ | Good |
  | B, B- | Fair |
  | C++, C+ | Marginal |
  | C, C- | Weak |
  | D | Poor |

  The FSR scale itself ends at D (Poor), the lowest rating. E, F and S are not ratings but "Financial Strength Non-Rating Designations": E = placed by court order into conservation or rehabilitation (or, without a court order, regulators have clearly acted to delay or limit policyholder payments); F = placed by court order into liquidation after a finding of insolvency; S = the outstanding FSR is suspended, e.g., when sudden, significant events make the rating impossible to evaluate. NR means not rated. Older guides (2009) listed E (Under Regulatory Supervision), F (In Liquidation) and S (Suspended) as ratings and grouped A++ through B+ as "Secure" and B and below as "Vulnerable". The current guide drops those group labels, though its definitions for Fair (B, B-) and below still say financial strength is "vulnerable" to adverse changes. [AM Best, Guide to Best's Financial Strength Ratings, Version 121719]
- An FSR is not a recommendation to buy a policy. It does not address claims-payment practices. [A.M. Best Guide, Rating Disclosure]

## Distribution Systems
- **Independent agency ("American agency") system**: independent contractor agents sell for one or more insurers on commission. The agent owns, uses, and controls the policy records and expiration data. [IRMI "independent agency system"]
- **Exclusive (captive) agency system**: the agent represents only one company or a group under common management. [IRMI "exclusive agency system"]
- **Direct writer**: the insurer uses its own employed sales force instead of independent agents or brokers. [IRMI "direct writer"]

## Producers and General Rules of Agency
- **Michigan definitions**:
  - An "agent of the insurer" sells, solicits, or negotiates as the insurer's representative. [MCL 500.1201(c)]
  - An "agent of the insured" is a producer who is not appointed by the insurer that writes the policy. That producer represents the insured, not the insurer. [MCL 500.1201(b)]
- A producer license by itself does not create any actual, apparent, or inherent authority to represent or commit an insurer. [MCL 500.1201(h)]
- **Express authority**: the powers spelled out in the agency contract. [IRMI "express authority"]
- **Implied authority**: actions beyond the written grant that are reasonably necessary for the agent's job. When the insurer does not object, the authority is treated as part of the contract. [IRMI "implied authority"]
- **Apparent authority (apparent agency)**: the agent has whatever power a reasonable person would assume, based on how the principal (insurer) presented things. It is tied to estoppel. [IRMI "apparent agency"; Restatement (Third) of Agency §2.03]
- **Fiduciary duty**: an agent is a fiduciary for all money received in the capacity of agent. Premiums and return premiums must be kept in a separate account and not commingled. Failing to remit on time is prima facie evidence of a fiduciary violation. [MCL 500.1207(1), (2), (8)]
- **Placing a refused risk**: if an agent places refused coverage through another agent, the agent must tell the buyer in writing that the coverage is not in effect until the buyer receives written evidence of insurance. [MCL 500.1207(12)]
- **Duty to advise (Michigan case law)**: an agent generally has no duty to advise an insured about the adequacy of coverage unless a "special relationship" exists. [Harts v Farmers Ins Exch, 461 Mich 1 (1999)] A special relationship can arise when the agent:
  - misrepresents coverage,
  - must clarify an ambiguous request,
  - gives inaccurate advice in response to an inquiry, or
  - assumes an additional duty.

  [Harts; later Mich Ct App decisions]

## Contracts: Elements of a Legal Contract
- **Michigan elements of a valid contract**: competent parties, a proper subject matter (legal purpose), legal consideration, mutuality of agreement (offer and acceptance), and mutuality of obligation. [AFT Michigan v Michigan, 497 Mich 197 (2015)]
- **Consideration in insurance**: the insured provides premium plus compliance with policy conditions. The insurer provides its promise to pay. Examples of the wording:
  - HO: "in return for the premium and compliance with all applicable provisions." [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Agreement]
  - WC: "In return for the payment of the premium and subject to all terms." [NCCI WC 00 00 00 C (Ed. 1-15), preamble]
- **Offer and acceptance**: the applicant's application is typically the offer. The insurer accepts by issuing the policy or a binder. A binder is temporary evidence of coverage until the policy is issued. [IRMI "binder"]

## Distinct Characteristics of an Insurance Contract
- **Adhesion**: the insurer drafts the policy, and the insured takes it or leaves it without bargaining over the wording. Courts therefore construe ambiguities against the drafter, the insurer. [IRMI "contract of adhesion"]
- **Aleatory**: an exchange of unequal value that depends on an uncertain event. An insured may pay premiums for years with no loss, or pay little and collect a large loss. [IRMI "aleatory contract"]
- **Unilateral**: only the insurer makes legally enforceable promises. The insured just has to meet conditions such as paying premium and reporting losses. [IRMI "unilateral contract"]
- **Conditional**: the insurer's duty to pay depends on the insured performing the policy conditions. For example, there is no duty to provide coverage if the insured's failure to perform duties after loss prejudices the insurer. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. C; IRMI "unilateral contract"]
- **Personal**: the contract insures a person, not the property itself, so it cannot be assigned without the insurer's written consent. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Secs. I & II Conds. E "Assignment"; NCCI WC 00 00 00 C, Part Six C]

## Legal Interpretations Affecting Contracts
- **Ambiguities**: ambiguous policy language is construed against the insurer, the drafter. [IRMI "contract of adhesion"; Wilkie v Auto-Owners Ins Co, 469 Mich 41 (2003)]
- **Reasonable expectations doctrine**: some courts read a policy to give the protection an insured would reasonably expect, even if the wording does not. [IRMI "reasonable expectations doctrine"] Michigan's Supreme Court declined to adopt this rule. It enforces unambiguous policies as written and resolves ambiguities against the insurer. [Wilkie, 469 Mich 41 (2003)] (See Flags.)
- **Indemnity**: the insured is restored to their pre-loss position, without profiting. Examples in the forms:
  - The CP form will "not pay you more than your financial interest." [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.4.d]
  - The HO form pays no insured more than that insured's interest at the time of loss. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. A]
- **Utmost good faith (uberrimae fidei)**: both parties are presumed to fully disclose all pertinent facts. [IRMI "uberrimae fidei"]
- **Representation**: a statement in the application that the applicant believes to be true. If it is false and the insurer relied on it, the insurer may have grounds to void the policy. A misrepresentation that is intentional and material can void the contract. [IRMI "representation"; "misrepresentation"; "material misrepresentation"]
- **Warranty**: a statement of fact about the risk, given by the insured, that will void the policy if untrue. Warranties are held to a stricter standard than representations. [IRMI "warranty"]
- **Concealment**: willfully holding back pertinent information even though the insurer did not ask about it. Concealment can void the policy. [IRMI "concealment"]
- **Fraud**: deception intended to cheat or mislead. Fraud in procuring insurance may void the policy. [IRMI "fraud"] Examples in the forms:
  - The CP form is void for fraud at any time, or for intentional concealment or misrepresentation of a material fact about the coverage, property, interest, or claim. [ISO CP 00 90 07 88, A]
  - The HO form (Section I) provides coverage to no insureds if, before or after a loss, any insured intentionally concealed or misrepresented a material fact, engaged in fraudulent conduct, or made false statements relating to the insurance, so one insured's fraud bars every insured. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. R] Under Section II, only the insured who did so loses coverage. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. II Cond. J]
- **Waiver**: the intentional, voluntary surrender of a known right. [IRMI "waiver"]
- **Estoppel**: a party cannot contradict its own earlier conduct when another party reasonably relied on it. Example: an insurer that repeatedly accepted late premiums may be estopped from canceling for late payment. [IRMI "estoppel"]
- Policies limit oral waivers:
  - HO: a waiver or change must be in writing by the insurer, and requesting an appraisal or examination does not waive the insurer's rights. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I & II Cond. B]
  - WC: terms may be changed or waived only by endorsement. [WC 00 00 00 C, General Section A]

## Flags
- **Reasonable expectations in Michigan**: the PSI outline lists this as a generic concept. Michigan's Supreme Court in Wilkie (2003) declined to adopt the doctrine. Exam answers should use the generic definition. We did not read the full Wilkie opinion; secondary sources consistently report the holding.
- **Elements of insurable risk**: the six-item list comes from a standard textbook (Rejda). Other texts phrase or number the items differently.
- **A.M. Best scale**: checked against the current AM Best guide (ambest.com/ratings/guide.pdf, Version 121719). Symbols A++ through D and their descriptors match the 2009 guide, but E, F and S are now non-rating designations, and the Secure/Vulnerable grouping no longer appears.
- **Harts duty-to-advise**: authorities disagree on whether the rule covers independent agents as well as captive agents. This is not tested.

# basics: Property & Casualty Insurance Basics

Outline weight: 20% (30 items). The Michigan-law sub-block (MPCGA, 500.2833 fire policy, 500.2123/.3020 cancellation, appraisal, TRIA, 500.1209, Essential Insurance Act) is covered by another file. Where ISO base-form wording is shown below, Michigan amendatory endorsements and statutes may change it.

## Insurable Interest
- **Definition**: the insured's financial or legal relationship to the subject of insurance. It usually comes from property rights, contract rights, or potential legal liability. [IRMI "insurable interest"]
- **Property insurance**: insurable interest must exist at the time of loss. Payment is capped at that insured's interest. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. A; ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.4.d]
- **Liability insurance**: insurable interest comes from the insured's potential legal liability. [IRMI "insurable interest"]

## Underwriting
- **Function**: deciding whether to accept a risk and, if so, how much insurance to write and at what rate. [IRMI "underwriting"]
- **Loss ratio** = incurred losses ÷ earned premium. Example: $50,000 ÷ $100,000 = 50%. [IRMI "loss ratio"]
- **Expense ratio** = underwriting expenses ÷ premium, most typically written premium (some calculations use earned premium). [IRMI "expense ratio"]
- **Combined ratio** = loss ratio (including loss adjustment expense) + expense ratio. Under 100% means an underwriting profit. [IRMI "combined ratio"]

## Hazards
- **Physical hazard**: a tangible condition that raises the chance of loss, e.g., poor housekeeping or inadequate lighting. [IRMI "hazard"]
- **Moral hazard**: comes from the insured's character or circumstances, e.g., dishonesty or an incentive to cause a loss. Underwriters avoid vacant buildings partly for this reason (arson for profit). [IRMI "moral hazard"]
- **Morale hazard**: the insured's indifference to loss because insurance will pay, e.g., carelessness. It reflects attitude, not integrity. [IRMI "morale hazard"]

## Negligence
- **Elements**: (1) a duty owed to the plaintiff, (2) breach of that duty, (3) proximate cause, (4) actual injury or damage. [IRMI "negligence"]
- **Contributory negligence (common law)**: any negligence by the plaintiff bars recovery. Many states have replaced it. [IRMI "contributory negligence"]
- **Comparative negligence**: the plaintiff's damages are reduced by the plaintiff's percentage of fault. [IRMI "comparative negligence"]
- **Assumption of risk**: a person who knowingly and voluntarily accepts a known risk cannot recover for the resulting injury. [IRMI "assumption of risk"]
- **Michigan rule (general tort)**:
  - A plaintiff's contributory fault does not bar recovery. [MCL 600.2958]
  - Damages are reduced by the plaintiff's percentage of fault. [MCL 600.2959]
  - If the plaintiff's fault is greater than the combined fault of all others (that is, more than 50%), economic damages are still reduced but noneconomic damages are not awarded. [MCL 600.2959]
  - In practice: comparative fault for economic damages, with a more-than-50% bar on noneconomic damages. [MCL 600.2959]
- **Michigan fault allocation**: each person's liability is allocated in direct proportion to their percentage of fault. Nonparties' fault is also considered. [MCL 600.2957(1)]
- **Michigan auto no-fault tort**: in a suit for noneconomic loss (or a nonresident's economic loss), damages are assessed on comparative fault, but no damages are assessed in favor of a party who is more than 50% at fault. [MCL 500.3135(2)(b)] The same rule applies to mini-tort vehicle damage claims. [MCL 500.3135(4)(a)]

## Damages
- **Compensatory damages** make amends for the actual loss. [IRMI "compensatory damages"] They come in two types:
  - **Special damages**: objectively measurable losses such as medical bills, lost wages, and loss of use. [IRMI "special damages"]
  - **General damages**: subjective awards for pain and suffering. [IRMI "general damages"]
- **Punitive (exemplary) damages**: awarded beyond compensation to punish wanton or willful conduct. [IRMI "punitive damages"]
- **Michigan on punitive damages**: they are generally not recoverable. They are available "only when expressly authorized by the Legislature." [Gilbert v DaimlerChrysler Corp, 685 NW2d 391 (Mich 2004); Casey v Auto Owners Ins Co, 729 NW2d 277 (Mich App 2006), via IADC Michigan Damages Compendium]
- **Michigan "exemplary" damages** are compensatory in nature: they compensate for injured feelings caused by the defendant's egregious conduct. [Peisner v Detroit Free Press, Inc, 364 NW2d 600 (Mich 1984), via IADC compendium]

## Absolute, Strict, and Vicarious Liability
- **Strict liability**: liability imposed without regard to fault or negligence. The classic example is a manufacturer or seller of a defective, dangerous product. [IRMI "strict liability"; LII Wex "strict liability" (animals, abnormally dangerous activities, products)]
- **Absolute liability**: liability without fault for ultrahazardous or abnormally dangerous activities (blasting, keeping wild animals). Exercising care is no defense. [Restatement (Second) of Torts §§519–520 (via LII/Justia); totalcsr insurance glossary] (See Flags.)
- **Vicarious liability**: a principal is liable for the acts of its agents, for example an employer for its employees acting in the scope of employment (respondeat superior). It can also apply to partners, independent contractors, and children. [IRMI "vicarious liability"; LII Wex "vicarious liability"]

## Causes of Loss (Perils): Named vs. Open
- **Named perils**: covers only the perils listed. The insured must prove a listed peril caused the loss. [IRMI "named perils coverage"]
- **Special (open) perils**: covers all causes of loss except those specifically excluded. It was formerly called "all risks." [IRMI "open perils"; "special perils"]

## Direct vs. Consequential (Indirect) Loss
- **Direct loss**: from direct physical damage to property. [IRMI "direct loss"]
- **Consequential (indirect) loss**: results from the direct damage, e.g., loss of rent, business income, or extra expense. [IRMI "consequential loss"]

## Blanket vs. Specific Insurance
- **Specific insurance**: each item (each building, or the personal property in a building) has its own limit. [ISO CP 03 21 10 12, definitions]
- **Blanket insurance**: one limit covers two or more items or locations. [ISO CP 03 21 10 12; IRMI "blanket limit"]
- With blanket insurance, coinsurance applies to the total value of all property under that limit. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.1.b and Example 3]

## Loss Valuation
- **Actual cash value (ACV)** is calculated one of three ways, depending on policy wording and state law:
  1. replacement cost minus depreciation,
  2. fair market value, or
  3. the broad evidence rule.

  [IRMI "actual cash value"]
- ACV is the default valuation in the BPP form. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.7.a]
- **Replacement cost (RC)**: the cost to replace with material of like kind and quality, with no deduction for depreciation. [IRMI "replacement cost"]
- **CP Replacement Cost optional coverage**:
  - It does not pay RC until the property is actually repaired or replaced, as soon as reasonably possible. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, G.3.d]
  - The insured may settle on an ACV basis and still claim the RC difference by giving notice within 180 days. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, G.3.c]
- **Functional replacement cost**: the cost of property that performs the same function with equal efficiency, even if it is not identical. An example is plaster walls replaced with drywall. [IRMI "functional replacement cost"]
- **Agreed value (CP optional coverage)**:
  - The Coinsurance condition is suspended until the agreed value expiration date. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, G.1.a–c]
  - Recovery is limited to the proportion that the limit bears to the Agreed Value shown in the Declarations. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, G.1.a–c]
  - If the agreed value is not extended, coinsurance is reinstated. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, G.1.b]
- **Stated amount**: commonly used for auto physical damage. The insurer pays the least of the stated amount, ACV, or the cost to repair or replace, so the stated amount is a ceiling, not a guaranteed payment. [ISO CA 99 28 "Stated Amount Insurance" (VA SCC filing, summary only); see Flags]

## Policy Structure (D-I-C-E + endorsements)
- **Declarations**: names the named insured, address, policy period, location, limits, and other information specific to the insured. [IRMI "declarations"]
- **Insuring agreement**: the insurer's promise to pay. It is broad, and exclusions and definitions narrow it. [IRMI "insuring agreement"]
- **Definitions**: words in quotation marks have the special meanings given in the Definitions section. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12 preamble]
- **Conditions**: the rules that govern the contract, e.g., cancellation, duties after loss, other insurance. [IRMI "common policy conditions"]
- **Exclusions**: the hazards, perils, property, or circumstances not covered. [IRMI "exclusion"]
- **Endorsements**: forms that change or add to the policy by broadening, restricting, clarifying, or adding parties. [IRMI "endorsement"]
- **Additional coverages and coverage extensions**: e.g., debris removal, preservation of property, fire department service charge, pollutant clean-up. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, A.4]

## Common Policy Provisions
### Insureds
- **Named insured**: anyone specifically designated by name in the policy. [IRMI "named insured"]
- **First named insured**: the one listed first in the declarations. They receive cancellation notices and return premiums and are responsible for paying premium. [IRMI "first named insured"] In WC: "the insured first named in Item 1 ... will act on behalf of all insureds to change this policy, receive return premium, and give or receive notice of cancelation." [NCCI WC 00 00 00 C, Part Six E]
- **Additional insured**: a person or organization added at the named insured's request, e.g., a landlord or project owner. [IRMI "additional insured"]

### Policy Period and Territory
- **CP**: covers loss commencing during the policy period and within the coverage territory: the USA (including territories and possessions), Puerto Rico, and Canada. [ISO CP 00 90 07 88, H]
- **HO Section I**: applies only to loss occurring during the policy period. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. Q]

### Cancellation and Nonrenewal (ISO base-form terms; Michigan statutes may override — see other file)
- **HO base form** [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Secs. I & II Conds. C, D]:
  - The insured may cancel at any time.
  - The insurer may cancel for nonpayment with 10 days' notice.
  - In the first 60 days of a new policy, the insurer may cancel for any reason with 10 days' notice.
  - After 60 days or on renewals, the insurer may cancel only for material misrepresentation or substantial change in risk, with 30 days' notice.
  - Return premium is pro rata.
  - Nonrenewal requires 30 days' notice.
- **Commercial form** [ISO-based commercial form text (Conifer/Accelerant MI forms reproducing IL 00 17 language)]:
  - The first named insured may cancel.
  - The insurer gives 10 days' notice for nonpayment and 30 days for any other reason.
  - If the insurer cancels, the refund is pro rata. If the insured cancels, the refund may be less than pro rata.

### Deductibles
- **CP order of operations**: (1) apply the coinsurance (or agreed value) reduction first, (2) then subtract the deductible, (3) then pay the result or the limit, whichever is less. The deductible applies once per occurrence. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, D]
- **HO**: if two or more deductibles apply to one loss, only the highest applies. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. B]

### Other Insurance
- **Nonconcurrency**: two or more policies cover the same exposure but do not match. IRMI's example is different inception and expiration dates, which can leave coverage gaps. [IRMI "nonconcurrency"]
- **Primary vs. excess**: primary insurance pays first. Excess insurance pays only above a stated amount or above the primary insurance. [IRMI "excess insurance"; "other insurance clause"] Under the CP form, other insurance that is not on the same plan and terms is treated as primary, and the CP pays only the excess, "whether you can collect on it or not." [ISO CP 00 90 07 88, G.2]
- **Pro rata (contribution by limits)**: each insurer pays the share that its limit bears to the total of all limits. [ISO CP 00 90 07 88, G.1; ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. G; IRMI "contribution by limits"]
  - *Worked example*: Insurer A has a $300,000 limit and B has $200,000, total $500,000. On a $100,000 loss, A pays 3/5 = $60,000 and B pays 2/5 = $40,000. [method per CP 00 90 G.1]
- **Contribution by equal shares (CGL)**: if all the other insurance permits equal shares, each insurer pays equal amounts until it has paid its limit or the loss is fully paid. If any other policy does not permit equal shares, the insurers contribute by limits. [ISO CG 00 01, Sec. IV Cond. 4.c "Method Of Sharing" (text via Law Insider sample); IRMI "contribution by equal shares"]
  - *Worked example 1*: $250,000 loss. A has a $100,000 limit and B has $300,000.
    - Equal shares: each pays $100,000, which exhausts A. B pays the remaining $50,000. Totals: A $100,000, B $150,000.
    - By limits instead: A pays 100/400 = $62,500 and B pays $187,500.
  - *Worked example 2*: $90,000 loss. A has $50,000 and B has $100,000. Under equal shares each pays $45,000, since neither limit is reached.
- **WC policy (Part One E and Part Two F)**: all shares are equal until the loss is paid. If one source is exhausted, the remaining shares stay equal. [NCCI WC 00 00 00 C]

### Limits of Liability
- **Per person**: the most paid for one person's injuries. Example: with a $50,000 per-person limit, claimants with $25,000 and $75,000 of damages get $25,000 and $50,000. [IRMI "per person limit"]
- **Per occurrence or accident**: the most paid for all injury and damage from one occurrence. [ISO CG 00 01, Sec. III.5]
- **Split limits**: three separate amounts per accident: per-person BI / per-accident BI / per-accident PD, e.g., 250/500/100. [IRMI "split limits"]
- **Combined single limit (CSL)**: one limit applies to BI and PD combined per accident. [IRMI "split limits" (contrast)]
- **CGL aggregates**: limits apply separately to each consecutive annual period. [ISO CG 00 01 04 13, Sec. III.1–7]
  - The *General Aggregate* is the most paid for Coverage C medical expenses, Coverage A damages (except products-completed operations), and Coverage B damages combined. [ISO CG 00 01 04 13, Sec. III.2]
  - The *Products-Completed Operations Aggregate* is a separate limit for Coverage A BI/PD within the products-completed operations hazard. [ISO CG 00 01 04 13, Sec. III.3]
  - Once the general aggregate is paid out, only products-completed operations coverage remains. [IRMI "general aggregate limit"]
- **Limits apply regardless of the number of** insureds, claims or suits, or claimants. [ISO CG 00 01 04 13, Sec. III.1]

### Property Limits and Restoration (Nonreduction) of Limits
- Property limits apply per item or location (specific) or across items (blanket). [ISO CP 03 21 10 12]
- **Inflation Guard**: automatically increases the limit by an annual percentage, prorated by days. Example: $100,000 × 8% × 146/365 = $3,200. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, G.2]
- **Reinstatement**: an aggregate limit is reduced by claim payments. Policies sometimes restore it automatically, with or without an extra premium, or only at the insured's request for extra premium. [IRMI "reinstatement"] The commonly taught rule is that property per-occurrence limits are restored after a loss while liability aggregates are eroded by payments (see Flags).

### Coinsurance (ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.1)
- **Formula**: (Limit carried ÷ (Value at time of loss × Coinsurance %)) × Loss − Deductible = payment. Payment is capped at the limit. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.1]
- Value is measured at the time of loss, not at inception. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.1.a]
- **Form Example 1 (underinsured)**: value $250,000, 80% coinsurance, limit $100,000, loss $40,000, deductible $250.
  - Required insurance: $200,000. Ratio: 0.50.
  - $40,000 × 0.5 = $20,000 − $250 = **$19,750**. The remaining $20,250 is not covered.
  - [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.1 Ex. 1]
- **Form Example 2 (adequate)**: same facts but a $200,000 limit. The limit meets the requirement, so the payment is $40,000 − $250 = **$39,750**. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.1 Ex. 2]
- **Form Example 3 (blanket)**: total value $250,000 at 90% requires $225,000. The limit is $180,000, so the ratio is 0.80.
  - The loss is $50,000: $50,000 × 0.8 = $40,000 − $1,000 deductible = **$39,000**.
  - [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.1 Ex. 3]
- **Extra worked example A**: value $400,000, 80% coinsurance, limit $240,000, loss $50,000, deductible $1,000.
  - Required insurance: $320,000. Ratio: 0.75.
  - $37,500 − $1,000 = **$36,500**.
  - [method per CP 00 10 F.1]
- **Extra worked example B (total loss)**: value $300,000, 80% coinsurance, limit $200,000, loss $300,000, no deductible.
  - Required insurance: $240,000. Ratio: 0.8333. $300,000 × 0.8333 = $250,000.
  - Payment is capped at the **$200,000** limit.
  - [CP 00 10 F.1 "or the Limit of Insurance, whichever is less"]
- **HO dwelling (different rule)**: full RC on Coverage A/B is paid only if the insurance is at least 80% of full replacement cost immediately before the loss. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. D.2.a]

### Vacancy vs. Unoccupancy
- **Vacant**: the building contains little or no furniture or personal property. **Unoccupied**: people are absent, even though contents remain. [IRMI "vacant"; "unoccupied"]
- **When a building counts as vacant under CP** [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.6.a]:
  - For a tenant, the "building" is its unit, and it is vacant when it lacks enough business personal property to conduct customary operations.
  - For an owner or general lessee, the building is vacant unless at least 31% of its total square footage is rented and used for customary operations, or used by the owner.
  - Buildings under construction or renovation are not vacant.
- **Effect under CP**: after more than 60 consecutive days of vacancy, there is no coverage for vandalism, sprinkler leakage (unless protected against freezing), building glass breakage, water damage, theft, or attempted theft. All other covered losses are reduced by 15%. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.6.b]
- **HO**: vandalism and malicious mischief, and glass breakage, are not covered if the dwelling was vacant more than 60 consecutive days before the loss. A dwelling under construction is not vacant. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Perils; Additional Coverage "Glass or Safety Glazing Material"]

### Named Insured Provisions
- **Duties after loss (HO)** [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. C]:
  - Give prompt notice.
  - Notify police of theft.
  - Protect the property from further damage and keep repair records.
  - Prepare an inventory.
  - Show the property, provide records, and submit to examination under oath.
  - Send a signed, sworn proof of loss within 60 days after the insurer's request.
- **Duties after loss (CP)**: substantially the same, with sworn proof of loss within 60 days after the insurer's request. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.3]
- **Assignment**: assignment of the policy is not valid unless the insurer gives written consent. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Secs. I & II Cond. E] In WC: "Your rights or duties under this policy may not be transferred without our written consent." [WC 00 00 00 C, Part Six C]
- **Abandonment**: CP says "There can be no abandonment of any property to us." HO says the insurer "need not accept any property abandoned by an 'insured'." [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.1; ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. K]

### Insurer Provisions
- **Liberalization**: broadened coverage adopted without additional premium applies automatically to existing policies. [IRMI "liberalization clause"]
  - CP: the revision must be adopted within 45 days before or during the policy period. [ISO CP 00 90 07 88, E]
  - HO: the implementation date must fall within 60 days before or during the policy period. It does not apply to general program revisions that both broaden and restrict coverage. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Secs. I & II Cond. A]
- **Subrogation**: after paying a loss, the insurer takes over the insured's right to recover from the party legally liable. [IRMI "subrogation"]
  - CP: the insured may waive recovery rights in writing before a loss. After a loss, the insured may waive only against another insured, a business owned or controlled by or owning the insured, or the insured's tenant. [ISO CP 00 90 07 88, I]
  - HO: an insured may waive in writing before a loss. Subrogation does not apply to Coverage F (Medical Payments) or to Damage to Property of Others. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Secs. I & II Cond. F]
- **Salvage and recovered property**: salvage is damaged property that can be saved. [IRMI "salvage"]
  - CP: if property is recovered after settlement, at the insured's option it is returned and the insured repays the insurer. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.5]
  - HO: the insured may keep or take back the property and the payment is adjusted. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. O]
- **Claim settlement options**:
  - CP: at the insurer's option it may pay the value, pay the cost to repair or replace, take all or part of the property at an agreed or appraised value, or repair, rebuild, or replace with like kind and quality. The insurer gives notice of its intentions within 30 days after the sworn proof of loss. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.4.a, c]
  - HO "Our Option": the insurer may repair or replace if it gives written notice within 30 days after the sworn proof of loss. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. I]
- **Loss payment timing**:
  - CP: within 30 days after the sworn proof of loss, provided there is agreement on the amount or an appraisal award. [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.4.g]
  - HO: 60 days after proof of loss, plus agreement, a final judgment, or an appraisal award. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. J]
  - Michigan statute also applies. Every fire insurance policy issued in Michigan (including a homeowners policy) must make the loss payable within 30 days after the insurer receives proof of the amount of loss, except as provided in 500.2845. The separate timely-payment rule (60 days after proof of loss, then 12% interest) is under Claims Handling below. [MCL 500.2833(1)(p); MCL 500.2006]
- **Duty to defend**: the insurer has the right and duty to defend suits seeking covered damages. [ISO CG 00 01 04 13, Sec. I Cov. A.1.a]
  - The duty is broader than the duty to indemnify: a potential for coverage is enough, and doubts are resolved for the insured. [IRMI "duty to defend"]
  - The duty ends when the applicable limit is used up paying judgments or settlements. [ISO CG 00 01 04 13, Sec. I Cov. A.1.a(2)]
  - CGL supplementary payments, including defense costs, are paid in addition to the limits. [ISO CG 00 01 "Supplementary Payments – Coverages A and B" (secondary confirmation)]

### Third-Party Provisions
- **Standard mortgage clause (HO)** [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. L]:
  - Coverage A/B losses are paid to the mortgagee and the insured "as interests appear," in order of precedence.
  - If the insured's claim is denied, the mortgagee's valid claim still stands if the mortgagee:
    - notifies the insurer of any known change in ownership, occupancy, or substantial change in risk,
    - pays premium due on demand, and
    - submits a sworn statement of loss within 60 days after notice of the insured's failure to do so.
  - The mortgagee gets at least 10 days' notice of cancellation or nonrenewal.
  - If the insurer pays the mortgagee and denies the insured, it is subrogated to the mortgagee's rights or may pay off the whole mortgage and take an assignment.
- **Standard mortgage clause (CP)** [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, F.2]:
  - Same structure. The mortgageholder keeps its right to payment even after starting foreclosure.
  - Cancellation notice: 10 days for nonpayment, 30 days for other reasons. Nonrenewal notice: 10 days.
- **Loss payable clause**: authorizes payment to a party other than the named insured that has an insurable interest, such as an auto lienholder or equipment lessor, "as its interest may appear." [IRMI "loss payable clause"]
  - Unlike the mortgage clause, the insurer usually owes the loss payee nothing if it can deny the insured's claim. [IRMI "loss payable clause"]
  - In HO, a loss payee is treated as an "insured" for the listed property and is notified in writing of cancellation or nonrenewal. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. S]
- **No benefit to bailee**: no person or organization other than the insured that has custody of covered property benefits from the insurance. [ISO CP 00 90 07 88, F] The HO version: the insurer will not recognize an assignment or grant coverage that benefits a person holding, storing, or moving property for a fee. [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. M]

### Indemnification Provisions
- **Hold-harmless (indemnity) agreement**: a contract in which one party agrees to take on certain legal liabilities of the other, e.g., a contractor indemnifying an owner. [IRMI "hold harmless agreement"] There are three forms:
  - Limited: covers only the indemnitor's sole negligence.
  - Intermediate: covers the indemnitor's sole or joint negligence.
  - Broad: covers even the indemnitee's sole negligence.

  [IRMI "hold harmless agreement"]
- Under the CGL, such assumed liability is covered if it is an "insured contract." [ISO CG 00 01 (insured contract concept; see CPP file)]

## Role of Applications and Binders
- **Application**: the applicant's statements, which are representations. Material misrepresentations can void coverage. [IRMI "representation"; "material misrepresentation"]
- **Binder**: a legal agreement issued by an agent or insurer that gives temporary evidence of insurance until the policy is issued. [IRMI "binder"] A binder should:
  - be in writing,
  - have a definite time limit,
  - name the insurer,
  - state the amount, the type of policy, and (for property) the perils insured.

  [IRMI "binder"]
- A producer can bind only within actual (express or implied) or apparent authority. A license alone creates no authority to commit an insurer. [MCL 500.1201(h); IRMI "express authority"]

## Insurance Claims Handling
- **Adjuster types** [IRMI "adjuster"; "independent adjuster"; "public adjuster"]:
  - *Staff adjuster*: an employee of the insurer.
  - *Independent adjuster*: works on contract for insurers or self-insureds, often in catastrophe or overflow situations.
  - *Public adjuster*: represents the insured, not the insurer, and is usually paid a percentage of the settlement. In Michigan, an adjuster for the insured may not charge more than 10% of the amount the insurer pays in settlement, and must contract in writing on a commissioner-approved form. [MCL 500.1226(3), (4)]
- **Typical process**:
  1. Notice of loss.
  2. Investigation: inspection, records, examination under oath.
  3. Sworn proof of loss.
  4. Coverage determination.
  5. Valuation, with appraisal if the amount is disputed.
  6. Payment or denial.
  7. Subrogation or salvage.

  [ISO HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Conds. C, F, I, J; ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.2–E.4]
- **Appraisal (ISO base wording)** [ISO CP 00 10 10 12, E.2; HO 00 03 05 11, Sec. I Cond. F]:
  - Each party picks an appraiser, and the two choose an umpire. Agreement by any two is binding.
  - Each side pays its own appraiser and splits the umpire and other expenses.
  - Under CP, the insurer keeps the right to deny the claim.
  - Michigan's statutory appraisal rule is in MCL 500.2833 (other file).
- **Michigan timely claim payment** [MCL 500.2006]:
  - The insurer must pay claims on a timely basis or pay 12% interest. Failure to do either is an unfair trade practice unless the claim is reasonably in dispute. [MCL 500.2006(1)]
  - The insurer must specify in writing what makes up a satisfactory proof of loss within 30 days after receiving the claim, unless the claim is settled within those 30 days. [MCL 500.2006(3)]
  - A claim is timely if paid within 60 days after the insurer receives proof of loss. Partial proof starts a separate 60-day clock for each supported part. [MCL 500.2006(3)]
  - Late benefits to the insured, or to a person directly entitled under the policy, bear simple interest at 12% per year from 60 days after satisfactory proof of loss. [MCL 500.2006(4)]
  - For a third-party tort claimant, 12% interest applies only if liability is not reasonably in dispute, the insurer refused payment in bad faith, and a court determined the bad faith. [MCL 500.2006(4)]
  - Interest is paid on top of, and at the same time as, the loss payment. If the loss exceeds the limits, interest is based on the limits. [MCL 500.2006(4)]
  - If the claimant rejected an offer and later recovers no more than that offer, no interest is due. [MCL 500.2006(4)]
  - Where 500.2006 is specifically inconsistent with the No-Fault Act (ch. 31) or the WDCA, it does not apply. [MCL 500.2006(6)]

  - CP 00 10 10 12 (copy at propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com)
  - CP 00 90 07 88 and CP 03 21 10 12 (Conifer Michigan hospitality forms)

  - CG 00 01 04 13 limits and duty-to-defend text (Argo annotated copy)
  - CG "Method Of Sharing" wording from a Law Insider reproduction of the standard ISO text. We did not view the full ISO PDF.
- **IL 00 17 11 98 Common Policy Conditions**: We could not get an ISO copy. The 10/30-day commercial cancellation notice is from a carrier form reproducing ISO language (Accelerant N CP 0169), confirmed by secondary summaries. Michigan statutes and amendatory endorsements govern actual notice periods (see the Michigan sub-block file).
- **Stated amount**: the "least of stated amount / ACV / repair cost" rule comes from secondary summaries of ISO CA 99 28. The Virginia SCC form PDF would not download.
- **Absolute vs. strict liability**: legal sources often use these terms interchangeably. Exam-prep convention uses "absolute" for ultrahazardous activities and "strict" for defective products. Michigan has not adopted Restatement §402A strict products liability as such; design-defect claims use a negligence risk-utility test [Prentis v Yale Mfg Co, 421 Mich 670 (1984)]. This is generic exam content; avoid Michigan-specific products questions.
- **Restoration of limits**: no single primary source covers the "property limits restored, liability aggregates eroded" convention. Use it as general guidance only.
- **Punitive damages**: Michigan citations are from the IADC Michigan Damages Compendium (2026), which quotes Gilbert (2004) and Casey (2006). We did not read the opinions themselves.
- **Michigan HO forms**: Michigan uses a "Special Provisions – Michigan" endorsement that can modify the HO loss payment, appraisal, suit, and cancellation terms quoted here.

Outline weight: 5% (8 items). Statute text is from legislature.mi.gov ("MCL complete through PA 103 of 2026").

## Michigan Workers' Disability Compensation Act (WDCA, MCL ch. 418)
### Exclusive Remedy (418.131)
- Workers' comp benefits are the employee's exclusive remedy against the employer for a personal injury or occupational disease. The only exception is an **intentional tort**. [MCL 418.131(1)]
- **Intentional tort**: the employer committed a deliberate act and specifically intended an injury. Intent is deemed if the employer had actual knowledge that an injury was certain to occur and willfully disregarded that knowledge. Whether an intentional tort occurred is a question of law for the court. [MCL 418.131(1)]
- For this section, "employer" includes the employer's insurer (and a self-insurer's service agent) when it furnishes, or fails to furnish, safety inspections or advisory services. [MCL 418.131(2)]
- **Losing the shield**: an employer that fails to secure compensation (§611), or violates the statutory-employer rule (§171), can be sued in a civil action despite §131. [MCL 418.641(2)] In that suit the employer may not use these defenses: the employee's negligence (unless willful), the fellow-servant rule, or assumption of risk. [MCL 418.141]

### Employers Covered (418.115)
- Private employers (other than agricultural) that regularly employ **3 or more employees at one time**. [MCL 418.115(a)]
- Private employers with **fewer than 3** employees if at least 1 has been regularly employed by that employer **35+ hours per week for 13+ weeks during the preceding 52 weeks**. [MCL 418.115(b)]
- **All public employers**, regardless of the number of employees. [MCL 418.115(c)]
- Agricultural employers of **3+ regular employees** paid hourly or salary (not piecework) and employed **35+ hours per week for 13+ consecutive weeks** during the preceding 52 weeks. [MCL 418.115(d)]
- Agricultural employers of **1+ employees** working **35+ hours per week for 5+ consecutive weeks** must provide medical and hospital coverage (§315) for work injuries. [MCL 418.115(e)]
- Any other private employer may voluntarily assume coverage. Buying a WC policy counts as assuming it, except for domestic and agricultural employees, who may be included by specific endorsement. [MCL 418.121]
- **Who is an employee** [MCL 418.161(1)(l)]:
  - Every person in the service of another under a contract of hire, including aliens and minors.
  - A minor under 18 who was illegally employed receives **double compensation**, unless permits were used fraudulently.
- **Exclusions by election or endorsement**:
  - A policy may, by endorsement, exclude named partners or the employer's spouse, child, or parent. [MCL 418.161(2)]
  - A corporate officer owning at least 10% of a corporation with no more than 10 stockholders may elect exclusion by written notice to the carrier. The same applies to an LLC manager-member owning at least 10% of an LLC with no more than 10 members. [MCL 418.161(3)–(4)]
- **Statutory employer**: a principal that hires an uninsured contractor is liable to the contractor's employees as if it employed them directly. The principal may then recover from the contractor. [MCL 418.171(1)–(2)]

### Covered Injuries (418.301)
- **Compensable injury**: a personal injury "arising out of and in the course of employment." It counts if work causes, contributes to, or aggravates pathology so as to create pathology medically distinguishable from what existed before. [MCL 418.301(1)]
- **Mental disabilities and aging conditions** (heart and cardiovascular conditions, degenerative arthritis) are compensable if contributed to, aggravated, or accelerated by employment "in a significant manner." [MCL 418.301(2)] Mental disabilities must also arise from actual events of employment, not unfounded perceptions. [MCL 418.301(2)]
- **Going to or from work**: an employee on the work premises within a reasonable time before or after working hours is presumed to be in the course of employment. [MCL 418.301(3)]
- **Social or recreational activity**: an injury from an activity whose major purpose is social or recreational is not covered. [MCL 418.301(3)]
- **Notice and claim deadlines** [MCL 418.381(1)–(3)]:
  - Notice of injury to the employer within **90 days**. Late notice is excused unless the employer proves prejudice.
  - Claim within **2 years** after the injury, or 2 years after death.
  - No payment for any period more than 2 years before the application for hearing.
  - No nursing or attendant care payment for any period more than 1 year before the application.

### Occupational Disease (418.401–.441)
- **Personal injury includes disease** caused by conditions characteristic of and peculiar to the employer's business. **Ordinary diseases of life** that the public is generally exposed to outside employment are not compensable. [MCL 418.401(2)(b)]
- **Hernia**: must be clearly recent in origin, result from a work strain, and be promptly reported. [MCL 418.401(2)(b)]
- **Police and firefighter presumption**: for covered members, respiratory and heart diseases that develop during active service are a "personal injury." They are presumed work-related in the absence of evidence to the contrary. [MCL 418.405(1), (3)]
- **Firefighter cancer presumption**: certain cancers in firefighters with 60+ months of service are presumed work-related. The claim is made against the Christopher R. Slezak First Responder Presumed Coverage Fund, and the presumption can be rebutted by evidence of substantial tobacco use. [MCL 418.405(2), (6)]
- **Disablement** from an occupational disease is treated as the happening of a personal injury. [MCL 418.411; .415]
- **Date of disablement** is the date the magistrate determines. [MCL 418.425]
- **No compensation** if the employee willfully and falsely represented in writing that he or she had not previously suffered from the disease. [MCL 418.431]
- **Proportional compensation** applies when non-compensable causes contribute to the disability. [MCL 418.431]
- **Last employer rule**: liability falls on the last employer in whose employment the disease was contracted. [MCL 418.435]
- **OD claim deadline**: within **2 years** after the employee (or dependents) knew, reasonably believed, or with ordinary diligence could have discovered that the disease or death was work-related. [MCL 418.441]

### Benefits Provided
- **Waiting period**: nothing is paid unless the incapacity lasts at least 1 week. Compensation begins on the **8th day**. If incapacity lasts **2 weeks or longer**, or death results, compensation is computed from the date of injury. [MCL 418.311]
- **Medical** [MCL 418.315(1)–(2)]:
  - Reasonable medical, surgical, hospital, and medicine costs, plus dental, crutches, artificial limbs, eyes, teeth, glasses, and hearing aids.
  - After **28 days** from the start of medical care, the employee may treat with a physician of their own choice by notifying the employer. The employer may petition to object.
  - Family-provided attendant care is capped at 56 hours per week.
  - Providers are paid the lesser of their usual and customary charge or the fee-schedule maximum.
- **Total disability**: **80% of after-tax average weekly wage (AWW)**, up to the maximum weekly rate under §355, for the duration of the disability. The conclusive presumption of total and permanent disability does not extend beyond **800 weeks** from the date of injury. [MCL 418.351(1); 418.301(7)]
- **Partial disability**: 80% of the difference between the pre-injury after-tax AWW and post-injury wage earning capacity, subject to the maximum. [MCL 418.301(8)]
- **Refusing work**: an employee who refuses a bona fide offer of reasonable employment without good cause gets no wage-loss benefits during the refusal. [MCL 418.301(9)(a)]
- **Death** [MCL 418.321]:
  - Wholly dependent survivors receive 80% of the after-tax AWW for **500 weeks** from the date of death.
  - A magistrate may extend payments for a dependent under **21** until that dependent turns 21.
  - Partial dependents receive a proportional amount.
- **Minimum benefits** [MCL 418.356(2)–(4)]:
  - Death benefit minimum: **50% of the state average weekly wage (SAWW)**.
  - Specific-loss minimum: **25% of SAWW**.
  - Total disability has **no minimum**.
- **Funeral and burial**: the reasonable cost, not more than **$6,000** or the actual cost, whichever is less. The employer also pays the expense of the last sickness. [MCL 418.345]
- **Maximum weekly rate**: set each January 1 for injuries in that year at **90% of the SAWW as of the prior June 30**, rounded up to the next $1. [MCL 418.355(2)]
- **2026 maximum: $1,201.00 per week**, based on a SAWW of $1,333.88, for injuries occurring in calendar year 2026. Check: 0.9 × 1,333.88 = 1,200.49, rounded up to $1,201. The 2025 maximum was $1,164. [Michigan LEO/WDCA "2026 Weekly Benefit Tables" (wca_2026_Rate_Book.pdf): "The 2026 maximum weekly compensation rate is $1201.00 based on a State Average Weekly Wage of $1333.88"; 2025 Rate Book: "$1164.00"]
- **Example**: an after-tax AWW of $1,000 gives $800 per week. An after-tax AWW of $1,800 would give $1,440, but for a 2026 injury it is capped at $1,201. [MCL 418.351(1); 418.355]

### Specific Loss Schedule (418.361) — compensation at 80% of after-tax AWW for the period listed
- **Weeks by member** [MCL 418.361(2)]:

  | Member | Weeks |
  |---|---|
  | Thumb | 65 |
  | First finger | 38 |
  | Second finger | 33 |
  | Third finger | 22 |
  | Fourth finger | 16 |
  | Great toe | 33 |
  | Other toe | 11 |
  | Hand | 215 |
  | Arm | 269 |
  | Foot | 162 |
  | Leg | 215 |
  | Eye | 162 |

- **Partial losses** [MCL 418.361(2)]:
  - Loss of the first phalange is ½ of the thumb, finger, or toe. Loss of more than 1 phalange is the whole digit.
  - Payment for more than 1 finger cannot exceed the payment for a hand.
- **Amputation thresholds** [MCL 418.361(2)]:
  - An amputation 6+ inches below the elbow is a "hand"; above that point it is an "arm."
  - An amputation 7+ inches below the tibial table is a "foot"; above that point it is a "leg."
- **Eye**: 80% loss of vision in one eye counts as total loss of that eye. [MCL 418.361(2)(l)]
- **Total and permanent disability** includes [MCL 418.361(3)]:
  - loss of sight of both eyes,
  - loss of both legs or both feet at or above the ankle,
  - loss of both arms or both hands at or above the wrist,
  - loss of any 2 of these,
  - permanent and complete paralysis of both legs, both arms, or 1 leg and 1 arm,
  - incurable insanity or imbecility,
  - permanent and total loss of industrial use of both legs, both hands, both arms, or 1 leg and 1 arm.
- **Loss of a second member** while the first is being paid: payments for the second loss start when payments for the first end. [MCL 418.361(4)]

### Second Injury Fund (418.521)
- An employee who already has a permanent loss of a hand, arm, foot, leg, or eye, and then loses another of these through a work injury, is **conclusively presumed totally and permanently disabled**. [MCL 418.521(1)]
- The **Second Injury Fund** pays T&P benefits starting when payments for the second loss end, after subtracting the weeks already paid for both losses. [MCL 418.521(1)]
- The SIF also reimburses carriers for certain differential and adjusted benefits, e.g., the one-time increase to up to 50% of SAWW after 2 years of continuous disability. [MCL 418.521(2); 418.356(1)]

### Self-Insurance and Group Self-Insurance (418.611)
- Every employer must secure payment of compensation either by:
  - **(a)** getting the director's authorization to self-insure, based on proof of solvency and financial ability (the director may require a bond, excess insurance, a letter of credit, or other security), or
  - **(b)** insuring with an insurer authorized to write workers' comp in Michigan.

  [MCL 418.611(1)]
- **Group self-insurance**: 2 or more employers in the same industry with **combined assets of $1,000,000 or more**, or 2 or more public employers of the same type, may pool their liabilities as a self-insured group with the director's permission. [MCL 418.611(2)]
- **Penalty for failing to insure**: a misdemeanor, with a fine up to $1,000, up to 6 months in jail, or both. Each day is a separate offense. [MCL 418.641(1)]

### Michigan Workers' Compensation Placement Facility (MCL 500.2301 et seq.)
- Every insurer authorized to write workers' comp in Michigan must participate. The facility's purposes are to provide coverage to anyone unable to get it through ordinary methods and to preserve price competition by encouraging maximum use of the normal private market. [MCL 500.2301]
- **Board of governors**: appointed by the commissioner (now the DIFS director) for 2-year terms. It has 9 members: 5 participating insurers, 2 policyholders, 1 licensed agent, and 1 member of the public. [MCL 500.2310]
- The plan of operation provides for:
  - servicing carriers,
  - producer **commissions of no more than 5% of total premium**,
  - three rating plans: **A** (poor loss experience or safety noncompliance, with surcharges), **B** (applicants who are self-insured or members of self-insured groups), and **C** (all others, with no surcharge).

  [MCL 500.2312(2)]
- **CAOM** (Compensation Advisory Organization of Michigan) handles Michigan WC data collection, experience modifications, and placement facility services. [caom.com]

## Federal Workers' Compensation Laws
- **FELA (45 USC 51–60)**:
  - Railroad common carriers in interstate commerce are liable in **damages** to employees injured or killed through the railroad's negligence, in whole or in part. [45 USC 51]
  - It is a negligence system, not a no-fault benefit system. [45 USC 51]
  - Contributory negligence does not bar recovery; damages are reduced in proportion. The employee bears no reduction if the carrier violated a safety statute. [45 USC 53]
- **US Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act (USL&HW / LHWCA, 33 USC 901 et seq.)**:
  - Every employer must secure compensation for covered employees. Compensation is payable **irrespective of fault**. [33 USC 904(a)–(b)]
  - It covers injuries on US navigable waters or on an adjoining pier, wharf, dry dock, terminal, building way, marine railway, or other adjoining area used for loading, unloading, repairing, or building vessels. [33 USC 903(a)]
  - It excludes a "master or member of a crew of any vessel"; those workers fall under the Jones Act. [33 USC 902(3)(G)]
- **Jones Act (now 46 USC 30104; formerly 46 App. USC 688, as cited in the PSI outline)**:
  - A **seaman** injured in the course of employment (or the seaman's personal representative) may sue the employer at law with a jury trial.
  - The federal laws governing railway employees (FELA) apply to these suits.

  [46 USC 30104(a); LII historical note]
  - The employee's contributory negligence reduces recovery but does not bar it. [IRMI "Jones Act"]
- **Employers liability exclusions**: the standard WC policy's employers liability coverage excludes work subject to LHWCA, FELA, and other federal comp laws, and bodily injury to a master or crew member of any vessel. These exposures need endorsements (e.g., Maritime WC 00 02 01; FELA WC 00 01 04). [NCCI WC 00 00 00 C, Part Two C.8–C.10; NCRB Basic Manual Rule 5]

## Workers' Compensation and Employers Liability Policy (NCCI WC 00 00 00 C, Ed. 1-15)
### General Section
- **The policy**: includes the Information Page and all listed endorsements and schedules. Its terms may be changed or waived only by endorsement. [WC 00 00 00 C, Gen. A]
- **Who is insured**: the employer named in Item 1. A partner is insured only as an employer of the partnership's employees. [Gen. B]
- **Workers compensation law**: the WC and occupational disease law of each state in Item 3.A. It excludes federal WC and OD laws and nonoccupational disability benefit laws. [Gen. C]
- **"State"**: any US state and the District of Columbia. [Gen. D]
- **Locations**: all workplaces in Items 1 or 4, plus all other workplaces in Item 3.A states unless otherwise insured or self-insured. [Gen. E]

### Part One — Workers Compensation Insurance
- **Covered injury**: bodily injury by accident (must occur during the policy period) or by disease. For disease, the employee's last day of last exposure must fall in the policy period. [Part One A]
- **We will pay**: "promptly when due the benefits required of you by the workers compensation law." **There is no dollar limit; coverage is statutory.** [Part One B]
- **Defense and costs**: the insurer has the right and duty to defend, and pays defense costs in addition to benefits. [Part One C–D]
- **Other insurance**: equal shares. [Part One E]
- **Payments you must make**: the employer reimburses the insurer for payments above regular benefits caused by:
  - its serious and willful misconduct,
  - knowingly employing someone in violation of law,
  - violating a health or safety law, or
  - discharging, coercing, or discriminating against an employee in violation of the WC law.

  [Part One F]
- **Recovery from others (subrogation)**. [Part One G]
- **Statutory provisions**: notice to the employer is notice to the insurer. The employer's bankruptcy does not relieve the insurer. The insurer is directly and primarily liable to injured workers. [Part One H]

### Part Two — Employers Liability Insurance
- **Covers** [Part Two A–B]:
  - damages for employee bodily injury arising out of and in the course of employment in an Item 3.A state,
  - third-party-over actions,
  - care and loss of services,
  - consequential bodily injury to a spouse, child, parent, brother, or sister,
  - dual-capacity claims.
- **Suits** must be brought in the USA, its territories or possessions, or Canada. [Part Two A.5]
- **Exclusions** [Part Two C]:
  - liability assumed under contract,
  - punitive damages for an employee employed in violation of law,
  - bodily injury to an employee knowingly employed in violation of law,
  - obligations under WC, OD, unemployment, or disability benefits laws,
  - intentionally caused bodily injury,
  - bodily injury outside the US or Canada (unless a citizen or resident is temporarily abroad),
  - employment practices claims,
  - LHWCA and other federal comp laws,
  - FELA,
  - master or crew of a vessel,
  - fines and penalties,
  - Migrant and Seasonal Agricultural Worker Protection Act damages.
- **Limits**: shown in Item 3.B. [Part Two G]
  - Bodily Injury by Accident — each accident.
  - Bodily Injury by Disease — policy limit (aggregate).
  - Bodily Injury by Disease — each employee.
- **Standard EL limits: $100,000 each accident / $500,000 disease–policy limit / $100,000 disease–each employee**. These may be increased. [NCRB (NC Rate Bureau) Basic Manual Rule 5, mirroring NCCI]
- **Defense**: in addition to limits; the duty ends when the limit is paid. [Part Two D–E]

### Part Three — Other States Insurance
- Applies only if states are listed in **Item 3.C**. [Part Three A.1]
- If the insured *begins* work in a 3.C state after the effective date, the policy applies as though that state were listed in 3.A. [Part Three A.2]
- If the insurer cannot pay benefits directly in that state, it reimburses the insured. [Part Three A.3]
- If the insured already had work on the effective date in a state not listed in 3.A, there is **no coverage there unless the insurer is notified within 30 days**. [Part Three A.4]
- The insured must "tell us at once" when work begins in a 3.C state. [Part Three B]
- Monopolistic state-fund states should not be listed in 3.C. [NCRB Basic Manual Rule 5]

### Part Four — Your Duties If Injury Occurs
- Tell the insurer at once. [Part Four]
- Provide immediate medical and other services required by law. [Part Four]
- Give names and addresses of injured persons and witnesses. [Part Four]
- Promptly forward notices and legal papers. [Part Four]
- Cooperate. [Part Four]
- Do nothing to interfere with recovery from others. [Part Four]
- Do not voluntarily make payments, assume obligations, or incur expenses except at your own cost. [Part Four]

### Part Five — Premium
- **Manuals**: premium is set by the insurer's manuals of rules, rates, rating plans, and classifications. [Part Five A]
- **Classification**: proper classifications are assigned by endorsement if the original ones do not fit. [Part Five B]
- **Basis**: premium = rate × premium basis, and remuneration (payroll) is the most common basis. [Part Five C]
  - The basis includes payroll of officers and employees, plus other persons whose injuries could make the insurer liable under Part One, such as uninsured subcontractors.
  - The contract price may be used if there are no payroll records.
  - Uninsured-subcontractor payroll is not counted if the insured proves those employers lawfully secured WC coverage.
- **Final premium**: the premium shown is an estimate. The final premium is based on actual exposure, determined by **audit**. [Part Five E, G]
- **Cancellation premium**: if the insurer cancels, the final premium is pro rata. If the insured cancels, it is more than pro rata (short rate). [Part Five E]
- **Audit window**: during the policy period and within **3 years** after it ends. [Part Five G]

### Part Six — Conditions
- **Inspection**: the insurer may inspect, but its inspections are not safety inspections and it does not warrant safety. [Part Six A]
- **Long-term policy**: a policy period longer than **1 year and 16 days** is treated as a new policy at each anniversary. [Part Six B]
- **Transfer of rights and duties**: requires written consent. If the insured dies and the insurer is notified within **30 days**, the legal representative is covered. [Part Six C]
- **Cancellation**: the insured may cancel with advance written notice. The insurer must give **at least 10 days'** advance written notice, but conflicting state law controls. [Part Six D] **Michigan**: the insurer must also file a notice with the Workers' Disability Compensation Agency at least **20 days** before a cancellation or termination takes effect, and the cancellation is not effective as to the insured's employees until 20 days after the agency receives that notice. [MCL 418.621(4)(g); Michigan Law Endorsement WC 21 03 04]
- **Sole representative**: the first named insured. [Part Six E]

### Voluntary Compensation Endorsement (WC 00 03 11 A, Ed. 8-91)
- **Purpose**: pays the equivalent of statutory benefits to employees whom the law does not require to be covered (listed in the Schedule), e.g., domestic, farm, or casual workers, or excluded officers and partners. [WC 00 03 11 A; NCRB Basic Manual Rule 5; IRMI "voluntary compensation endorsement"]
- **Before payment**, the claimant must:
  - give a written release of the employer and insurer,
  - transfer recovery rights to the insurer, and
  - cooperate.

  [WC 00 03 11 A, D]
- If the claimant sues instead, the duty to pay ends; the employee may effectively choose common-law remedies. [WC 00 03 11 A, D; IRMI]
- Part Two employers liability applies as though the schedule state were listed in 3.A. [WC 00 03 11 A, F]

## Premium Computation
- **Manual premium** = (payroll ÷ 100) × rate for each classification. Rates are stated "per $100 of payroll." [NCRB Basic Manual Rule 5; IRMI "manual premium"]
  - Example: $250,000 payroll at a $4.00 rate = 2,500 × $4.00 = $10,000.
- **Experience modification**: compares the employer's actual losses with the expected losses for its classes and payroll. [Pinnacol; EMC] The mod is usually based on 3 years of experience, excluding the most recent completed policy year. [Pinnacol; EMC]
  - A mod of 1.00 is average, under 1.00 is a credit, and over 1.00 is a debit. [Pinnacol; EMC; IRMI "experience modification"]
  - Standard premium = manual premium adjusted by the experience mod. Example: $10,000 × 0.85 = $8,500. [IRMI "standard premium"]
- **Premium discount**: a volume discount reflecting lower expense ratios on larger policies. It is applied after experience rating, to standard premium, at graduated rates. In the Michigan Placement Facility, discount applies only when total standard premium exceeds $10,000 (0% on the first $10,000, then 5.1% / 6.5% / 7.5% bands). Voluntary-market insurers file their own discount tables. It is not available with retrospective rating. [CAOM MWCPF Basic Manual (Ed. 09/26) Rule Seven C-D and Premium Discount Table; MCL 500.2406(3); IRMI "premium discount"]
- **Audit**: payroll audit after expiration sets the earned (final) premium. [IRMI "payroll audit"; WC 00 00 00 C Part Five G]

## Flags
- **2026 maximum weekly rate ($1,201; SAWW $1,333.88)**:
  - michigan.gov blocked direct download (403), so the figure comes from the search-engine snippet of the official 2026 rate book plus a law-firm post dated Feb. 12, 2026. The figure is consistent with the §355 formula.
  - It applies to injuries in calendar 2026 only, and the maximum changes every January 1.
  - Avoid exam reliance on the specific dollar figure.
- **EL standard limits** (100k/500k/100k): verified in the NC Rate Bureau Basic Manual (an NCCI-style manual). Michigan WC data and experience mods are handled by CAOM, not NCCI. The NCCI policy form is used nationally, but we did not see a Michigan-specific manual rule on EL limits.
- **Premium discount threshold**: the $10,000 threshold and the 5.1% / 6.5% / 7.5% bands come from the CAOM Michigan Workers' Compensation Placement Facility Basic Manual (Ed. 09/26), Rule Seven D and its Premium Discount Table. IRMI's generic definition uses $5,000, and thresholds vary by state. Michigan voluntary-market insurers file their own rates and rating systems, including discount tables (MCL 500.2406(3)).
- **Experience mod period** (3 years, excluding the most recent year): from carrier explainers (Pinnacol/EMC), not CAOM's Michigan plan. The Michigan specifics were not verified.
- **WC policy cancellation**: the policy says at least 10 days' notice, "changed to comply" with any conflicting state law. Michigan law requires every WC policy to provide that the insurer files notice with the Workers' Disability Compensation Agency at least 20 days before a cancellation or termination, and that the termination is not effective as to employees until 20 days after the agency receives the notice (MCL 418.621(4)(g); Michigan Law Endorsement WC 21 03 04).
- **Jones Act citation**: the outline cites 46 USC 688, which was recodified in 2006 as 46 USC 30104.
- **Placement Facility statutes**: the board composition and rating plans are from MCL 500.2310 and 500.2312. We could not retrieve every section of 500.2301 et seq. (several section numbers returned no text, possibly repealed or unused).
