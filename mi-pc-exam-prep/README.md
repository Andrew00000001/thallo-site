# Michigan P&C Exam Prep

Interactive study app for the Michigan Property & Casualty Producer/Solicitor exam (PSI Series 16-81: 150 items, 2.5 hours, 74% to pass).

Open `index.html` in a browser. It is one self-contained file: the lesson narration and drawings are built into the page, so nothing else needs to sit next to it. When the page is served together with its `lessons/` folder (for example from a web server or the published app), lessons play the full-quality recordings in `lessons/*.hq.webm`; opened on its own, the page plays its built-in compact copy and the browser's own voice reads the activity lines. Progress is saved in that browser; see **Saving progress** below.

Four tabs: **Today**, **Learn** (video lessons, study guide, memory aids, key numbers), **Practice** (questions, flashcards, math lab, games) and **Mock exam**.

- **Today**: daily plan with due reviews and new questions, streak, daily goal, XP and levels, badges, the picture puzzle, exam countdown, projected score, a "Where to focus" panel (weakest topics, why you miss, how sure you were against how often you were right) and a mastery map of every outline section whose squares read without color (empty, slashed for missed, half filled, filled, filled dark; tap one for details and a practice button). **Learn first**: until you finish a lesson, a "Start here" card leads with the first lesson instead of questions. After that, new questions in the daily session come only from lessons you have finished (each lesson's own end-of-lesson questions plus the bank's questions on the same subtopics in its section), and questions you answered before come back for review when they are due. Settings can switch the session to new questions from every section, mixed like the exam.
- **Video lessons**: 24 narrated whiteboard video lessons (about 188 minutes of narration), one or more per outline area. Terms are drawn and acted out by a recurring cast in everyday situations. Each lesson stops for things to do: story choices (pick what the character does next; a wrong pick plays out what goes wrong and lets you try again), quick checks, swipe cards, sorting, tap-to-explore drawings and sliders that run the exam math. Lessons work by ear alone: the narration says every fact shown on screen, the narrator reads each activity aloud (the question and choices, each card, item or spot, the options, and the feedback after an answer), and activities move on by themselves once answered, so you can listen without reading (press H to hear an activity again). Stars, sound effects (can be turned off), captions with the speaker's name, a transcript, speed control and resume; each ends with exam-style questions from the bank (Check yourself) and, where the bank has more on the same subtopics, an offer of more practice. Lessons 11, 12, 13 and 18 show an edition note where a newer form changed a figure they use.
- **Study guide**: a "how to study" page, then one section per exam-outline area plus exam-day logistics, each with a list of the topics on the page and a "Continue reading" link back to where you stopped. Every fact cites its source.
- **Memory aids**: 125 memory hooks (each lists the exact facts it stands for), 30 confusion pairs with 154 drills, and 18 picture summaries.
- **Practice**: 682 questions in PSI item format, from what you've learned (finished lessons, the default while learning first), one section or all sections, with spaced repetition, confidence ratings (confident-but-wrong answers come back first), recall-first mode and a "why did I miss it" journal. When a newer form edition or a scheduled change makes a figure different, the answer shows a "Watch for" note.
- **Games**: Lightning round, Survival, Boss battles per section, Match (36 sets) and the picture puzzle.
- **Picture puzzle**: every question you answer right (in the daily session, Practice, Lightning round, Survival, Boss battles or a mock exam) uncovers one of 16 jigsaw pieces over a hand-drawn picture. Once 3 pieces show you can guess, on Today, from Games (under Practice), or from the small puzzle beside practice questions (on phones, under a right answer). A right guess scores 1 point plus 1 for each piece still hidden, minus 1 for each wrong guess (never less than 1). The category is always shown; after a wrong guess with at least 8 pieces showing, the letter count; after two wrong guesses with at least 12 showing, the first letter. A near miss ("building" for a house) gets a nudge and costs nothing, as does a two-way guess ("cat or dog"), which is turned back. Typing slips ("torando") still count; a different word that names something else ("hot dog") does not. Once every piece is uncovered you can show the answer instead, for no points. Solved pictures go to your collection, some with a related flashcard. There are 40 pictures; after the last one they come around again in a new order, for points but no XP.
- **Math lab**: freshly generated problems with worked solutions for coinsurance, homeowners replacement cost, actual cash value, other-insurance sharing, split limits, Michigan PIP work loss, workers' comp premium and loss ratio.
- **Mock exam**: 150 questions weighted like the PSI outline, 2.5-hour timer, section-by-section results. Optionally adds 10 unscored experimental questions, as PSI may add one to ten; they use time but don't count. You can stop an attempt without scoring it, and an attempt whose time ran out while you were away can be scored as it stands or discarded.
- **Flashcards**: 225 cards on a review schedule. **Key numbers**: 233 limits, deadlines and amounts, with notes where a newer edition changed a figure.
- **Saving progress**: progress is kept in the browser and, when the app is opened from its claude.ai link while signed in, in your Claude account, so it follows you between browsers and devices. On the website, signing in with Google does the same, with the progress kept for your Google account. The two copies are merged, never overwritten: an answer, flashcard, lesson, badge, puzzle piece or mock exam recorded anywhere is kept, and a fresh or out-of-date copy can't wipe a fuller one. Changes are sent to the account within about 20 seconds (lesson play positions within about a minute), and also when you switch away from or close the page; failed saves are retried. The Today screen shows the save status, and a notice explains what to do when progress isn't reaching your account (for example when the page was opened from a downloaded file). **Backup code** (Today → Settings) copies all progress as text to keep or to load in another browser; loading a code adds its progress to what's there. **Reset progress** erases progress everywhere it's saved.
- **Colors** (the Colors button at the top; on a first visit Today also asks): five color sets made for long study sessions. Dusk, the default, is soft dark umber with warm light text, about 3% of white's brightness. Sage is muted gray-green paper with dark green-gray text, about 40%. Sepia is dim tan paper, about 60%. Night is warm charcoal, about 1%. Auto follows the device: Sepia by day, Night in dark mode. No color set has a white or blue background, and every text color meets WCAG AA contrast. The choice is saved with your progress.

Content was checked in October 2026 against the Michigan Compiled Laws (legislature.mi.gov), PSI's Series 16-81 outline and candidate bulletin, Michigan DIFS bulletins, FEMA/NFIP rules, U.S. Treasury (TRIA), and ISO/NCCI policy forms. Edition-dependent amounts name their form edition. In November 2026 the homeowners material was compared with ISO's 2022 forms (the study guide shows the 2011 and 2022 amounts side by side, and each question names its edition), and the commercial auto, CGL and crime material with ISO's newer editions. Each area was researched, then fact-checked separately; the memory aids and lessons were then cross-checked against the study guide and questions. Every lesson was written, then checked by separate reviewers for facts, teaching and visuals, fixed, and re-checked until clean; each lesson's `.trace.md` maps its claims to sources and `.fixlog.md` records the review.

## Rebuild

`content/` holds the source data (`content/learn/` the memory aids, `content/lessons/` the lesson scripts with their trace, coverage and fix logs, `content/puzzles.json` the picture puzzles in play order: each a drawing from the prop library with its answer, accepted and near-miss guesses, look-alikes that don't count, category and an optional related flashcard; `content/lessons/notes.json` edition notes shown with lessons whose narration predates a form change), `lessons/` the narration (full-quality 32 kbps Opus masters `LXX.hq.webm` with the activity lines after the main narration, the compact 8.5 kbps Opus copies of the main narration that get embedded in the page as the offline fallback, sized so the page stays under 16 MB, timing and transcript checks), and `build/` the build scripts, player and drawing engine:

```
RES=content LEARN=content/learn LESSONS=content/lessons AUDIO=lessons ICONS=build/icons VALIDATOR=build WB=build/wb python3 build/build.py out.html
```

The script writes the page body; `index.html` wraps it in a full HTML document. The build stops on a puzzle with a bad or repeated id, no answer, no drawing, an unknown drawing part, malformed word lists or a flashcard link that doesn't exist. Questions and key-number rows may carry a `note` (shown as a "Watch for" note), and a question's `noShuffle`, when set, is kept as written. In the study-guide markdown, `{{2022: ...}}` marks a 2022-edition figure, a `{.class}` line before a table gives it that class with one row per line, and lines between ```` ```html ```` and ```` ``` ```` are copied as they are. The build refuses lesson audio that no longer matches its script, embeds each lesson's compact Opus copy (`EMBED_AUDIO=0` links the full-quality files instead), and writes `out.html.hq.json`, the list of full-quality files to publish next to the page (`HQ_AUDIO=0` leaves them out). The lesson data and audio are compressed inside the page (deflate and base85) and unpacked when it opens.

**Website** (mi-pc-exam-prep.firebaseapp.com, the version saved to home screens): `python3 build/site.py` builds it into `site/` (not committed): the same app, with the lesson audio as files in `site/lessons/` (the compact copies as `LXX.lq.webm`, the full-quality files as `LXX.hq.webm`) instead of inside the page, plus `site-src/`: Firebase loading (`firebase.html`), `cloud.js`, which gives the app the same saving service it gets inside Claude, built on Google sign-in and one Firestore document per person (`progress/<user id>`, the progress stored as one JSON string), the page head, and the icons and manifest. Deploy with the Firebase CLI, signed in to the Google account that owns the project: `firebase deploy --only hosting` (from this folder; `firebase.json` sets the cache headers: the page is always rechecked, lesson audio is cached for a week).

Lesson scripts follow `build/BRIEF2.md` and must pass `python3 build/validate2.py <lesson.json>`. Drawings are drawn by `build/wb/sketch.js` from the prop library in `build/wb/props_*.js` (`build/wb/PROPS_CATALOG.md` lists every prop). Narration is made by `build/narrate2.py` with the open Kokoro-82M voice model (ONNX, Apache-2.0; set `TTS_MODEL` to a folder with `model.onnx` and `voices.npz`), one voice for the narrator and one per cast member. The activity lines the narrator reads aloud come from the activity text itself (`build/clips.py`; a lesson's `clipsay` field overrides how a line is said) and are recorded after the main narration in the full-quality file. Every line, activity lines included, is transcribed back with faster-whisper (`small.en`, MIT) and compared word for word with the script; reviewed transcription differences are listed in `lessons/accepted.json`.

Bundled third-party code and fonts: rough.js (MIT, `build/wb/lib/rough.LICENSE`), the Patrick Hand font (SIL Open Font License 1.1, `build/wb/lib/hand.LICENSE`), tiny-inflate (MIT, `build/wb/lib/tiny-inflate.LICENSE`) and Lucide icons (ISC, `build/icons/LICENSE`).
